The truck came at seven. By noon the cave was empty, and it echoed in a way I
wasn't ready for. You live somewhere long enough that the walls stop being walls
and start being the backdrop to everything — and then you take the backdrop down
and put it in a box labeled **KITCHEN — FRAGILE**.

## The lesson kept arriving late

Here's what I noticed. Every one of us learned the same thing on this move, but
we each got there on our own clock.

- Mama knew it before the truck even showed up: *a home is the people, not the
  place.* She's said it for years. This time I finally believed her.
- Babu figured it out around box number thirty, when he stopped asking "why do
  we have to go" and started asking "can I pick my room."
- Nanu got there last, at bedtime, when she realized her ceiling was a different
  ceiling and cried about it for ten minutes and then fell asleep fine.

I got there somewhere on the highway, watching the old mountain shrink in the
mirror.

## What I'd tell past me

Change doesn't wait for everyone to be ready. It just starts, and the family
catches up to it one person at a time. Your job isn't to make everyone arrive
together. Your job is to keep the fire lit while they're still walking.

We unpacked the kitchen first. We always do. Warm food in a cold house is how
this family says *we're going to be okay here.*
