Seven in the morning. The light in our bathroom is the aggressive kind, the sort
that finds every part of your face you had not agreed to look at yet.

I am standing on a stool. I need the stool. We are not going to discuss the
stool.

Two sinks. Three Yetis. The math has never worked and nobody has offered to fix
it.

## The two of them at the sink

Papa Yeti brushes like the toothbrush owes him money. Mama Yeti brushes like
somebody is scoring her on it. Water goes everywhere. There are droplets on the mirror, on
the counter, and on me, and none of the three of us has ever mentioned it.

Same two Yetis. Same forty seconds of scrubbing. Every single morning of my
entire life.

Then, halfway through, Papa Yeti leaned over and nuzzled Mama Yeti. Toothbrush
still in his mouth. Foam on his chin. She made a sound like a kettle that is pleased about
something.

I kept brushing. Somebody in this family has to hold the routine together.

## What I noticed, with foam on my cheek

Here is a thing I have never said out loud.

Nobody taught me this. There was no lesson. No chart on the cave wall with
stickers on it. Nobody sat a very small mint-green Yeti down and explained why a
mountain full of yak butter and momo is hard on the teeth.

What happened was this. They were up at the sink doing something, together,
looking awake about it, and I wanted in. So I dragged over a stool. I got a tiny
yellow toothbrush. I copied the motion, got it wrong, got foam on my cheek, and I
have been doing it every morning since. That is the whole origin story. There is
no wiser version of it.

> They never once told me to brush my teeth. They just did it in front of me
> until it belonged to me too.

By the end all three of us were going at once, in the mirror, roughly in time,
like the world's smallest and mintiest band. Bubbles in the air. Everybody
suspiciously wide awake for that hour.

I want it on the record that I did not enjoy this. I am simply reporting the
facts.

## The Yeti Way

**Small Yetis do not do what you tell them. We do what you do at seven in the
morning when you think nobody is watching.**

A note for the grown-ups, and I am aware it is mostly grown-ups reading this.

You can explain a good habit to a kid for a year and get nothing back. Then you
go and do the thing yourself, loudly, badly, with foam on your face, and the kid
drags a stool over and joins you. We were never listening. We were watching the
whole time.

Which cuts both ways, so I will mention it. I am also watching how the two of you
are with each other over the sink at seven in the morning.

The nuzzle counted too.

Anyway. Brush your teeth. ❄️❤️
