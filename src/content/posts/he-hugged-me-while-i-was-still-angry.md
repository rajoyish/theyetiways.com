Eleven ice blocks. Stacked since morning, squared at the corners, the good clear
ones from the deep end of the cave where the light comes through them blue.

Then a crash like the mountain clearing its throat.

I came around the corner already loud. Paws on my hips, brow down, doing that
frosty huff through my nose that means I have decided to be weather about
something. And there in the wreckage stood Babu Yeti, mint-green and very small, eyes
the size of dinner bowls, looking at me the way you look at an avalanche you
personally started.

## Then Papa Yeti hugged me mid-sentence

I had a speech. It was organized. It had a section on how long stacking takes and
a section on paws and where we keep them.

Papa Yeti walked in and put his arms around both of us before I got to the first
word of it.

He did not defend Babu Yeti. He did not tell me to calm down, which is the fastest
known way to make a She-Yeti less calm. He just gathered us up, all that blue fur
closing in from both sides, and made a low rumbling chuckle in his chest that you
feel more than hear.

The tense air in the cave went flat, like somebody had opened a window.

## You cannot stomp inside a hug

There is no room. I tried for about two seconds out of principle, because I had
done the work of getting angry and I wanted some return on the investment.

Then Babu Yeti giggled into my fur and my shoulders came down without consulting
me.

That is the part that stayed with me. Not the ice, not the mess. The fact that my
body let go of the fight the moment somebody held it, which means the fight was
never really about the blocks. It was about a morning of work turning into a pile
of glass in one second, and having nowhere to put that feeling except at a child.

> The blocks took a morning to stack and an afternoon to stack again. Nobody in
> this cave has ever rebuilt an afternoon of being shouted at.

We stood there in the cold with the shards around our feet, the three of us
folded into one shape. Babu Yeti nuzzled in. Papa Yeti beamed like a Yeti who
had gotten away with something, which he had.

## The Yeti Way

**Somebody has to break the tension, and it does not have to be the one who is
right.**

I was right. The blocks were mine, the morning was mine, and Babu Yeti had
been told about running near them. Being right is a warm place to stand and I could have
stood there all day.

Papa Yeti gave up the argument nobody was having and went straight to the thing
underneath it, which was a small green Yeti who already knew, and a pink one who
needed somewhere to set down the anger before it landed on him.

So we clean up together now. That is the rule in this cave. You can be furious
and still be held. The two have never once cancelled each other out.

Break the tension first. Sweep the ice second. ❄️❤️
