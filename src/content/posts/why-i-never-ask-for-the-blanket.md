Magh wind, the kind that finds one gap in the cave door and sits there whistling
about it all night. Under a blanket heavy enough to flatten a small yak, I should
have been the warmest creature on this mountain.

I was not. I was cold, and I was awake, and beside me my husband's face was lit
up blue by a little glowing rectangle he was tapping like it owed him money.

## What I did not do

I did not ask him for anything.

Understand that first, because it is the whole story. I did not tap his shoulder.
I did not say *the blanket, please.* Yetis from my side of the valley are raised
to think that is rude, and I am also stubborn, which is a separate condition I
like to dress up as manners.

What I did instead was shiver at a volume. I turned over. I rustled the bedding.
I sighed in a minor key. If a scientist had been in the room, he would have
written in his notebook: *subject is performing winter.*

The tapping continued. Fast, cheerful, endless. Somewhere out there a stranger
was being disagreed with, and my toes were going numb for the cause.

## Then the typing stopped

That is the part I actually noticed. Not the click of the phone locking. The
absence of the clicking.

He did not ask me what was wrong, which I am grateful for, because I would have
said *nothing is wrong*, and then we would both have been lying to each other at
midnight in the cold.

He just pulled the blanket back across both of us and tucked it in at my
shoulder. He reached over and switched on the little star lamp, the amber one
that turns a cave into a den. The room went from blue light to gold light. So did
I, honestly.

> Asking gets you the blanket. Being noticed gets you the whole warm Yeti.

Then he pulled me into his side, and I went, no ceremony about it, fur against
fur. I let out a sigh I had not planned on making. Outside, the wind carried on
with its complaint to nobody.

## The Yeti Way

**Being loved is not being answered. It is being noticed before you had to open
your mouth.**

I should say the honest part too. Silent shivering is not a system. It is
certainly not a test, though I have known Yetis who ran it as one, and I would
not recommend that to anybody. Make love prove itself in the dark often enough
and you end up with a tired partner and a cold bed.

So I try to pay the difference in the morning. He put the phone down before I
asked him to. Over tea, where it counts, I tell him that I noticed that he
noticed.

That is the trade, and it is a fair one. He looks over without being asked. I
say it out loud instead of making him guess at the temperature of his own wife.

Neither half works alone. A Yeti who never looks up leaves you cold. A Yeti who
never says anything leaves him guessing in the dark.

Notice first. Then take the warm side together. ❄️❤️
