The hearth was doing the slow crackle it does after the second log. Snow sat
in the cave mouth. I sat on the floor with both paws on my belly, which is
where they go when I am not planning anything.

Then the footsteps. Padded, unhurried, from the left. That is the sound of
Mama Yeti bringing me something, and I have learned to look up before I know
what it is.

A wooden plate, piled high. Burgers on the bottom, fries on top, and the smell
arrived a full step before she did.

## The paw goes up

She held it out with the smile she uses when she already knows the answer and
is hoping I forgot it. Hopeful. Eager. Eyebrows up.

I have said no to that plate before. I have said it with a speech about the
knees and the ridge and how a Yeti my size should climb it without stopping
twice. She has heard every version. She kept bringing the plate, because a
speech is only air, and a plate is a plate.

So this time I did not make a speech. I shook my head, slowly, so the fur
rustled. I put one paw up, palm out. The plate creaked a little in her grip.

And she tilted her head. That is the tilt that means the music has stopped and
nobody knows what comes next. She looked at the plate. She looked at me. The
plate had not changed. I had.

## The crunch

I leaned past the burgers and reached for the bowl on the flat rock, the one
that has sat beside the fire all season looking decorative. I took out one
apple. Red, cold, heavy in the paw the way a good apple is.

I gave it a polish on my chest. Then I bit it.

You have to understand what that sounds like in a stone cave. The crunch went
off the walls and out into the snow. It probably reached the yak. I closed my
eyes, because it was good, and because I wanted her to see my face while it
was good.

When I opened them I held the apple up toward her like I had won something.
Her eyes went very wide. They went from the apple to the plate in her paws and
back again, and I could see the two things arguing behind them.

> I had told her about apples a hundred times. The apple had never said a word
> for itself until then.

Her face softened. She nodded once, lowered the plate, and slid it away along
the floor until the fire lit only empty stone. I winked. She reached into the
bowl, turned an apple in her paw, and smiled at it the way I had.

## The Yeti Way

**You do not talk someone into a better habit. You take the bite where they
can hear it.**

I spent a year explaining apples to Mama Yeti. She spent a year nodding and
bringing fries, and she was right to, because there is nothing to follow in an
explanation. You cannot sit shoulder to shoulder with a speech.

The crunch was different. It was a thing she could watch me enjoy. Nobody
lowers a plate for an argument. People lower plates for a face that looks
happy about what it just did.

We sat by the hearth with an apple each. We bit at the same moment, two
crunches, one echo, and leaned against each other while the fire did its slow
thing.

Say less. Crunch louder. ❄️❤️
