Morning mist drifted over the terrace. Snow-dusted pines framed the stone, and the peaks caught the dawn. On a mossy pedestal, the elder red panda tapped the bronze bowl with a wooden hammer.

A clear chime echoed across the courtyard, met by the melody of a solo Daegeum. Beside me, Mama Yeti settled into stance, pink fur glowing in the cold. Babu Yeti stood centered, his mint-green tuft trembling in the breeze. We bowed together. As the sound faded, I recalled the old wisdom: mastery begins with a humble bow, grows through patient practice, and culminates in the joyful unity of heart and breath.

## The Wobble in the Tiger Stance

I stepped into Jumbi with the weight of a blue boulder. When Papa Yeti demonstrates Taegeuk Seven Jang, he brings dignity to the stone. I dropped into tiger stance, drove my palm heel forward, and prepared to look carved from granite.

Granite never has to balance on one leg.

Beside me, my wife moved like water. Mama Yeti floated through her knife-hand blocks without a sound. Babu Yeti chose courage over stillness. In his tiger stance, his small brow furrowed in fierce focus. On the front kick, his little paw snapped waist-high, but the landing took two hops to keep from rolling into the moss. He stared ahead as if the peaks were scoring his form.

I tried to anchor us with bulk, repeating the teaching: like the ancient mountain, let your focus be unwavering, your discipline silent, and your spirit rooted in harmony. Yet the stiffer I stood, the more my balance drifted. Babu Yeti was not fighting the stone. He was breathing with the flute.

## The Shout Across the Peaks

We crossed into scissor blocks, forearms meeting the cold air with clean thuds. We drove knee strikes upward and sank into cross stances, double uppercuts cutting through snow.

By the crescent kicks, the rhythm took over. Babu Yeti slapped his palm with a pop, spun into horse riding stance, and drove his target elbow home. The apprentice red panda leaned forward, bushy tail twitching in delight.

In deep horse riding stances, we drew fists to ribs and threw a unified side punch. A thunderous Kihap tore from our chests, rolling off the stone flags. The elder red panda stepped down to spar. We traded blocks, evading kicks to land side by side in horse stance, punching the sky with wide grins.

> True strength is not found in standing alone, but in rising together with immovable balance and shared purpose.

## The Yeti Way

**Harmony is not forced by one heavy anchor; it lives where everyone breathes together.**

I began the dawn convinced my role was to hold the terrace down, standing so rigid that nothing could sway us.

Stiff branches snap in the wind. Mama Yeti brought the grace that kept us fluid. Babu Yeti brought the joy that kept us light. The form held because we shared every breath from the first bow to the final shout.

Lower your guard. Find the common rhythm. Bow with an open heart. ❄️❤️
