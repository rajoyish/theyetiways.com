The stew landed on the table with a thud you could feel in your knees. Steam
went straight up into the lantern light. Whatever Mama Yeti had been doing at
that pot since the sun left the ridge, it had worked.

I had the big wooden spoon in one paw and the fork in the other before the plate
stopped moving. I was not thinking about anything. I was aiming.

## The paw

Then a pink arm came across the table like a gate coming down.

No words. Just the arm, and the face above it, half-lidded and entirely serious.
I froze with the cutlery still up, like a Yeti caught stealing his own dinner.

The cheerful little tune I had going in my head cut out. Something in my grip
knocked the wood, one flat clack, and after that there was nothing at all. Cave
silence. Stew steam.

I lowered my arms. I set the spoon down. It made a hollow clink I would describe
as dejected, because I was.

## What she was actually doing

Out came the ice slab. Palm-sized, polished blue, frost glowing across the face
of it. She carved that thing herself and she does not hand it to anybody.

She leaned over my dinner. Click. Moved. Click. Tipped her head, went around the
far side of the plate, got low so the steam caught the light. Click click click.

Here is what I was doing while she worked, and I am admitting it because it
turned out to be wrong. I was sitting there being hungry at her.

Then I looked up at her face.

She was not admiring herself. She was checking whether the picture had caught
what she had made, and she was worried it had not.

> She was not delaying my dinner. She was trying to keep hold of an evening that
> was about to get eaten.

She lowered the slab. One small nod, one flick of the paw. Go.

I went. Loudly. Eyes shut, mouth full, a fair amount of it in my fur. When I
came up for air she had her chin on her paw, watching me with the face she gets
when the verdict is in and the verdict is good.

## The Yeti Way

**The pause before the meal is not somebody stealing your dinner. It is somebody
asking you to notice it with them.**

I made that stew wait, and I would rather not have. The food was hot and I am no
saint about hot food. But Mama Yeti stood at that pot half the evening while I
sat here with my cutlery ready, as if dinner had grown on the table by itself.

A picture is a small tax. It costs one held breath. What it buys is a night she
gets to keep after the plate is empty and the pot is scrubbed and I have
forgotten the whole thing.

So now I wait. Spoon down, both paws where she can see them. I hold still until
the nod comes, and the nod always comes.

The stew has never once been colder for it. ❄️❤️
