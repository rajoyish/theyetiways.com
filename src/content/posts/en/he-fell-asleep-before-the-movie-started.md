Babu Yeti went down at eight. Flat on his back in the little stone bed at the
rear of the cave, one mint-green arm hanging off the side, that curly tuft of fur
aimed at the ceiling.

Which meant the mountain was ours. I turned the lanterns down to a glow, dragged
the heavy woven blanket onto the stone couch, and clicked the remote. The TV
hummed awake and threw soft light across the cave wall. Papa Yeti sat down next
to me grinning like we had gotten away with something.

## He made it to the studio logo

Not the opening credits. The logo. The little spinning one before the movie
remembers to start.

One second he was upright and cheerful. The next he tipped sideways like a sack
of barley, eyes shut, tuft of blue fur collapsing against the cushion.

Then the snoring. Soft, rhythmic, completely shameless, laid right over the
soundtrack like it had been mixed in on purpose.

## I had a whole speech ready

I want to be honest about the first two seconds. There was a small hot flicker in
my chest, because I had waited all week for this, and it was a good movie, and I
had made the good popcorn.

Then I actually looked at him. Not at the ruined evening, at him. At a Yeti who
had emptied himself out completely by eight in the evening and still came and sat
down beside me, because being next to me was the last thing on his list and he
wasn't going to skip it.

> He didn't fall asleep on our night. He fell asleep on the safest spot he could
> find, and that spot happened to be me.

So I let the speech go. I pulled the blanket up over the two of us, guided that
heavy blue head down off the cushion and into my lap, and let the movie run for
nobody.

The lanterns glowed. The snow kept doing whatever snow does out there. Somewhere
behind us Babu Yeti sighed in his sleep, and in front of me a grown Yeti drooled
gently on my knee, and I sat in the middle of the two of them feeling like the
richest creature on the mountain.

I have no idea how the movie ended. Neither does he. We are going to watch it
again next week and I predict the exact same outcome.

## The Yeti Way

**The evening you planned is not the evening you were given, and the given one is
usually better if you stop arguing with it.**

Tired people do not need you to be disappointed in them. They already are. Every
household has somebody running on empty, and the kindest thing in the room is
whoever decides not to make that a problem tonight.

So keep the blanket handy. Let the plan lose. A quiet cave, a sleeping kid, and a
snoring Papa Yeti is not a failed movie night. That is the movie.

Turn the lanterns down. Let him sleep. ❄️❤️
