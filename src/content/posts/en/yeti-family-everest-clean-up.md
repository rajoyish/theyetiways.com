Morning on the high col of Mount Everest. A bite-your-snout wind swirling over
ancient ice, turning the low sun into blinding diamonds. At this altitude,
every breath counts, but a blue Yeti in his prime feels like a walking tower.

Beside Mama Yeti and Babu Yeti, I watched spindrift dance across the ridges. My
chest was puffed out, sapphire fur catching the pale gold light, ready to show
off how easily a giant strolls through clouds.

## The rust beneath the snow

Babu Yeti toddled ahead, his mint-green curl bobbing until he stopped near a
shelf of receding blue ice. He tilted his round head and poked at the crust with
one paw.

A sudden gust flapped a scrap of tattered yellow nylon with a hollow whip.

Beneath the thaw lay no frozen flower. Decades of expeditions had left their
mark: buckled ladders, crushed tents, and tarnished oxygen canisters rusted into
the shale. Babu Yeti pulled his paws to his tummy in disbelief.

Then came the crunch of boots on crust. Three Sherpa clean-up workers crested
the ridge, hunched under burlap sacks. They halted, hands on knees, breath
misting white in the thin altitude.

## Lifting the mountain

My wife did not hesitate. Mama Yeti touched my elbow, her warm eyes meeting
mine before nodding toward the debris. You do not watch tired people carry the
roof of the world alone when your shoulders are the size of boulders.

Babu Yeti scrambled forward, scooping loose cans into a burlap sack with a
determined grin. Mama Yeti gathered an armful of heavy canisters, cradling them
like firewood.

I stepped to the main tangle of bent ladders and frozen canvas. I dug my heels
into the shale and heaved. The iron tore free with a solid crunch, and I hoisted
the burden across my shoulders.

The Sherpas froze in astonishment as their load rose into the morning sun.

> True strength isn’t measured by how much you can lift, but by how much burden you can lift from the shoulders of others.

We marched down the trail together. At the lower camp, we set the bundles onto
the snow terrace with a soft puff of powder. The workers pressed their mittened
palms together in a deep namaste of gratitude. Back on the summit ridge, golden
light washed the slopes spotless. Babu Yeti gently stroked the pure snow, smiling
as Mama Yeti whispered that the greatest gift you can offer the earth is leaving
it just as pure and untouched as the day you first looked upon it.

## The Yeti Way

**Protecting our shared sanctuary is never a solitary journey: it begins the
moment we lend our hands to care for the world we love.**

It is easy to strut across peaks feeling mighty with big bones and thick fur.
But muscle alone does not make a guardian. Power matters only when it eases the
load for someone whose knees are shaking.

The mountains do not belong to the ones who climb them; they belong to all of us
who share their quiet breath. When you leave a place cleaner than you found it,
you honor the ground that holds you.

Pack out what you carry. Lighten the trail for the traveler behind you. ❄️❤️
