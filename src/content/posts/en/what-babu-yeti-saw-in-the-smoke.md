The fire was still two ridges out when we came down onto the ash slope, and I
was doing what I always do in a crisis. Reading it.

Wind direction. Which pines had already gone. Mama Yeti had a paw on my arm and
her eyebrows drawn together, watching embers drift past like the worst snow on
the mountain. Babu Yeti hopped a step ahead of us, and I did not check what he
was looking at, because I was busy being the one who understood the situation.

## The log came down

The doe went first, off the far side of the slope at a dead run, straight past
us, embers kicking off the ash behind her hooves. Then the smoke took her shape
and she was gone.

Her fawn did not keep up. It stumbled and fell short beside the big charred log,
a burning branch cracked above us and dropped in a spray of sparks, and the log
shifted. It came down across the fawn's hind legs with a heavy wooden thud. One
small frightened bleat, and then only the fire.

All three of us stopped where we stood. I will be honest about what I did next,
which was nothing. I stood on the ash doing arithmetic about weight and heat.

## The smallest paw pointed first

Babu Yeti tugged my arm and pointed down the slope, his mouth shut in a tight
line. He said nothing, because nothing needed saying. He had been watching the
fawn since we walked in. I had been watching the fire.

So I ran. I got both paws under the charred wood at chest height, Mama Yeti
braced the near end, and we drove it up while the flames climbed along the far
side. Babu Yeti was already crouched at the gap with his paws out, and the fawn
scrambled into them before the log was clear of the grass.

Then Mama Yeti knelt. She opened both paws low over the scorched ground, the
light between her palms and the earth turned a cool silver-blue, and the air over
the slope dropped cold. Where that light reached, the fire sank to embers. Even
in the fiercest fire, compassion is the quiet force that turns destruction into
peace. I have watched her do it twice in my life and I still have no better words
for it.

> True strength does not just withstand the flames. It reaches into the smoke to
> protect the vulnerable.

The doe came back out of the haze, unharmed. The fawn wobbled up and went to
Babu Yeti instead, and the two of them stood forehead to forehead with their eyes
closed while pale ash came down like slow snow.

## The Yeti Way

**No burden is too heavy when lifted by courage, and no heart is too small to
lead the way.**

I lifted the log, and I will take the credit for the wood. I am the biggest Yeti
on that slope, so the wood was my job. I will not take the credit for the rescue.

That started when somebody small looked at the one who could not run instead of
at the thing that was burning. I had the arms. Babu Yeti had the eyes.

So now, when the mountain goes wrong, I look where Babu Yeti is looking. He
stands lower than I do. He sees what is down there.

Go toward the smoke. Take your family with you. ❄️❤️
