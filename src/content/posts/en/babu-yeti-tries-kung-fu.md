Papa Yeti does kung fu on the terrace before breakfast. I do not know who told
him to. Nothing has ever attacked this cave except the cold.

I sat on the stone a step to his right. That is my seat. The snow was the quiet
kind that has decided to stay, and somewhere behind us a drum started up, low
and steady, with strings plucking over it.

He pulled both fists in to his hips. His feet went wide. Then he sank, and kept
sinking, until a Yeti the size of a boulder was sitting on air.

## The nod

Front knee bent, back leg straight, one big palm pushing forward so slowly I
checked whether the valley was moving instead. A leg crossed behind, a fist went
out, a forearm came up like a roof, and he dropped back into the wide stance
with the other fist out.

Then he looked sideways at me and nodded. The stone was right there. My legs
were attached to me. So I got up. I planted my feet as wide as they go, pushed
one paw out, and I am told my tongue was out too.

## The part where I sat down

He went up on one leg, arms out flat, and held it like the mountain was holding
him. I went up on one leg and the mountain let go of me. I tipped, hopped, and
lived.

Then he dropped. One leg stretched along the terrace, one arm sweeping low over
it, fur going whoosh. I slid my leg out. My leg is short. The stone was not. I
went down on my bottom in a puff of snow and blinked at the sky.

He did not stop the form. He went on up into the next stance, one knee high,
arms level, calm as a lantern, and left a space on the stone beside him.

He has a saying for this. *A strong foundation is not built by never falling,
son. It is built by rising stronger every single time you hit the snow.* I had
heard it before. It sounds different with snow actually on you.

> He did not wait for me. He just did not go anywhere without me either.

So I got up. Brushed the snow off. Lifted my knee, wobbled, tuft shaking, and
held it. When he dropped again, I dropped again, and this time the stone and I
came to an arrangement. I stayed up.

Feet together. Arms up in a slow circle. Fist into palm. A small bow. The drum
let go of the beat and we stood facing each other like two Yetis who had agreed
on something.

Then he reached over and ruffled the tuft on my head, and I shut my eyes and
leaned into it, because that is the real last move.

## The Yeti Way

**You do not learn the standing up part without doing the sitting down part
first.**

I thought the plop was the mistake. It was not. The plop was the stance, one try
early. Papa Yeti has done that form more mornings than I have been alive, and
there is snow on his bottom somewhere in all of that too.

He says true strength is taught with patience, but the greatest bond is forged
when we stand side by side through every stumble. He says protecting yourself
starts in the mind, but the courage to keep trying comes from a father's belief
in your heart.

I do not understand all of that yet. I understood the nod, the space beside him,
and a grown Yeti not stopping. The stone did the rest.

Fall down. Brush off. Lift the knee again. ❄️❤️
