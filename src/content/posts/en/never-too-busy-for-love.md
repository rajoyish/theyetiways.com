The cave was doing its quiet thing. The lantern was low, the rain was somewhere
off past the ridge, and the only sound on the whole mountain was me hitting keys
like the work would run off if I stopped.

I was not having a bad day. I was having a full one. There is a difference, and
somewhere in the middle of that morning I had lost the ability to feel it.

## The mug

Something warm landed next to my paw.

A soft thud, stone on stone. My husband had come in behind me without a word,
put a mug of hot tea down where my hand would find it, and gone back out.

He did not ask what I was working on. He did not tell me to take a break, which
is the fastest way in the world to make a person not take one. He did not stand
there waiting to be thanked for it.

He set it down. He left.

I kept typing anyway. I want that in the record. Then the steam got up my nose
and my whole face went traitor on me.

## What the tea knew

I took a sip and turned away from the screen, and that turn was the entire trick
of it. You cannot drink something that hot in a hurry. A mug of tea makes you
stop whether you had planned to or not. Papa Yeti knows this about tea. I think
he knows it about me.

> He did not interrupt my work. He interrupted my forgetting.

So I got up out of that chair and I leaned over the desk and put my arms around
him, badly, at an angle no one would design on purpose. He closed his eyes. He
went soft into it and let out a sigh that sounded like a Yeti setting down a
whole day.

Neither of us said anything. Outside, the rain came back over the ridge. The
laptop fell asleep on its own, which felt like it agreed with us.

## The Yeti Way

**Love is not something you schedule for later. It is something you carry into
the room while the other one is busy.**

Busy is not the enemy. Busy is just what a life looks like when people need
things from you. The trouble is that busy is loud, and it convinces you that
every warm thing has to wait until the quiet comes. The quiet does not come.
Nobody on this mountain has ever finished everything.

I could have made that tea myself. That is the part I keep turning over. The
kettle was ten steps away and I would not have gone, because getting up felt
like losing. It took someone else deciding I was worth ten steps.

So he did not wait for my quiet. He walked a hot mug into the middle of my noise
and trusted it to do the work.

You do not need the right moment. You need a mug, a pair of paws, and the
willingness to be ignored for a bit.

Bring the tea. The rest sorts itself out. ❄️❤️
