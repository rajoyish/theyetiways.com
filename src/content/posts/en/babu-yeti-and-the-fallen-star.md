Stars are not supposed to be heavy. This one was the size of a momo and it sat
in my left hand like it had opinions. It was warm, too, the way chiya is warm
when Mama Yeti hands it over before it has cooled.

I landed on the ice lotus with a clear crystal ping and a puff of frost around
my feet. The garden above the clouds was quiet the way a place with no grown-ups
in it is quiet. Moonlight on one side. Amber lamps on the other. One small star,
very clearly not where it belonged.

## The moth knew the way

Tavi Moon Moth was hovering at the bridge like it had been waiting a while. One
foreleg pointed across the sky at the star arch, at the single empty socket near
its lower end. Nobody points at a hole like that unless they want it filled.

So I ran. Ice taps under my feet, tick tick tick, the whole length of the bridge.
Then the mushroom caps, yellow, purple, blue, pink, each one squashing under me
with a fat springy thump. I giggled. I would like it noted that this was not
part of the plan. It was just very bouncy.

The last cap ended at a big golden star lying flat as a raft. I stepped on. It
hummed. I held the small one a little tighter.

## A click in the sky

The clouds were in the way, which is what clouds are for. Then Oru Cloud Ray
swept one fin through them with a rush of air, and the curtain opened like a
blanket pulled back in the morning. The glider slid straight through.

Up close, the socket sat right at chest height. I did not smile. I pressed my
mouth shut and lined the star up the way Papa Yeti hangs a yak bell on its hook,
slow and serious. Click.

> The whole sky was missing one small piece, and for once the smallest one of us was holding it.

The arch lit up end to end. I let out a sigh that went all the way to my knees.
Tavi Moon Moth landed on my empty hand. Oru Cloud Ray gave a small dip of the
head, which from a ray is a lot.

Then we kept going, out along the Starway, a long trail of stepping stones over
the cloud sea. Far ahead, four Lumen Grazers drifted across it, huge and slow,
humming like our cave does when the wind is right. Tavi Moon Moth folded its
wings and went still. So did I. Two of them passed close enough to curl the
clouds with their fins. Then they sank into the cloud sea and were gone.

## The Yeti Way

**You have to put down what you are carrying before anything can land in your
hand.**

All night my left hand had one job. Hold the star. Don't drop it. Don't let a
bounce shake it loose. I got so good at holding on that I forgot hands do
anything else.

The moth only came after the click. The grazers only came close after I stopped
wiggling. Neither of those was in the plan either.

Finish the job properly. Then open your hand and wait. ❄️❤️
