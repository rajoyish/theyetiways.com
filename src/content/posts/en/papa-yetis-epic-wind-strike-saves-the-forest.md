The morning sun cut amber beams through the canopy, and the path smelled
of damp pine needles. Nothing on the mountain demanded my attention, so I had
time to supervise.

Babu Yeti was crouched flat on his haunches in the dirt, nose inches from the
trail. A line of glossy black ants marched over a root, and he watched every
single one as if it carried a secret. Two white rabbits dozed in the ferns.
Mama Yeti leaned her shoulder against mine, and the whole forest was at rest.

## The roar on the right

Then the dry brush caught like pine needles in a stove.

A sharp crackle ripped along the edge of the path, followed by a roar of orange
flames leaping through the briars. The two white rabbits bolted from the ferns
with frantic thuds against the dirt. Babu Yeti tumbled back onto his paws, eyes
round as river stones, mouth open in a gasp.

My first thought was panic. But Mama Yeti stepped between our child and the
fire, gathered the two trembling rabbits against her pink fur, and shielded them
against her legs. She did not shout. True strength isn't just about the power to
fight the fire: it is having the courage to protect the gentle things that
cannot protect themselves. Her calm gave me an anchor.

## One steady breath

I planted my right foot on the packed dirt and dropped into a horse stance. My
knees creaked, but my center held.

Flames reared up, throwing embers into the canopy. I pulled my fist back to my
hip and drove my knuckles into the empty air. It touched no wood. A howling vortex
of mountain wind tore off my fur, churning up dust and needles, slamming
straight into the wall of fire.

> Stand firm in the face of the storm, for when your purpose is love, even the
> fiercest blaze can be quieted by a single, steady breath.

The fire hissed, choked on the blast, and collapsed into white smoke. The heat
died into cool air. I drew my fist back, waiting for another flare. None came.
The rabbits slipped from Mama Yeti's paws, twitched their ears, and hopped back
into the ferns.

## The Yeti Way

**Peace returns not when the chaos disappears, but when we slow down enough to
find wonder in the smallest wonders of life.**

I stood on the path with fists clenched, waiting for the next disaster. That is
what we do when we think strength means bracing for impact. We keep our fists up
even when the sky is clear.

Mama Yeti laid a warm paw over my knuckles until my fingers loosened. Babu Yeti
was not trembling. He had wandered to an ancient hollow tree trunk, found a dry
twig, and was gently tapping the wood beside a shiny dark green beetle.

He was not reliving the blaze. He was watching six tiny legs climb over moss.
The danger had passed, so he let it go. We cannot protect our family if we
carry the heat of the fire long after the forest has cooled.

Breathe out the smoke. Look for the beetle in the hollow. ❄️❤️
