Snow coming down all night, the wind doing its low howl past the mouth of the
cave, and the three of us in one heap under a blanket built out of about six
yaks' worth of fur.

I was asleep. Deeply. Professionally. Mama Yeti on one side of me, Babu Yeti
curled up in the middle, all of us breathing in the same slow rhythm like one
large animal with three faces.

## Then I rolled over

That was the whole crime. A tug, a shift, a Yeti getting comfortable.

The blanket came with me. All of it. Every corner of it dragged into a warm
cocoon wrapped around exactly one Yeti, and the sound it made was the soft heavy
rustle of a thing leaving somebody else.

I did not wake up. That is the part I keep chewing on. Behind my back the draft
came in off the snow and went straight across the two of them, whistling, and I
slept through it wearing the face of a Yeti who takes care of his family.

Mama Yeti woke up shivering. She did not elbow me. She did not haul the blanket
back, which she had every right in the world to do.

She looked over at Babu Yeti trembling beside her, scooped him up, and wrapped
both arms tight around him. She put her own fur between my son and the cold.

## What I woke up to

That was the view when I finally blinked my eyes open. My wife holding our boy
against her chest, the pair of them shaking, and me rolled up in every scrap of
warm on the mountain.

> I had not taken the blanket from strangers. I had taken it from the only two
> I would walk into a storm for.

So I did the only thing there was. I reached over, gathered that heavy blanket
in both paws, and spread it back over them. I tucked the edge under Babu Yeti so
the draft could not find a seam. Then I pulled them both in against me until we
were a heap again.

Nobody said anything. The wind kept on outside, and it stayed out there.

## The Yeti Way

**Most of the cold you cause, you cause in your sleep.**

No Yeti wakes up planning to take the warm off his family. It is not a decision.
It is a roll, a habit, a slow drift toward your own comfort while everyone you
love adjusts around you and says nothing about it. You can be a good Yeti and
still be the reason somebody is cold.

Which is why waking up is the whole job. Not being perfect through the night.
Just opening your eyes at some point, looking at the shape of the bed you are
actually in, and noticing who is holding who together.

Mama Yeti did not fix it by taking anything back. She fixed it with the only
blanket she had left, which was herself. I would like to be that fast one day.
For now I am the one who wakes up second and spends the rest of the night
tucking edges.

Roll over. Look. Then cover them back up. ❄️❤️
