Our grain sack was so light I could have held it open with one finger. I held
it with both hands anyway, the way you hold something you hope will change its
mind. It did not. A few grains of rice rattled at the bottom, embarrassed to be
seen.

Next door, Tashi Marmot was polishing a gold coin on his crimson vest and
holding it up to the sun. Mama Yeti looked at his painted house, then at our
wooden bowls, and sighed. She was right, I thought. I am not a clever Yeti. So
I decided to buy some cleverness with the only two copper coins we had.

## A coin for each lesson

Babu Yeti carried the money box out hugged against his tummy, as if it held the
family jewels. In a way it did. He tipped both coins into my hand, and I walked
off down the mountain road feeling brave and only slightly foolish.

The first coin went to Blue Magpie, at a stall of buttons and coloured glass.
It raised one wing like a schoolteacher and told me to always do what
respectable folk tell me. Then it tucked my coin under that same wing. I
thanked it. I meant it. I would like that noted before anyone laughs.

The second coin went to Dolma Yak, at a tea stall high on the windy pass. She
nudged a cup of hot tea toward me and said one thing. Always tell the Queen the
truth. I put a hand on my chest and kept that one close.

## The bundle that smelled

Three Grand Peacocks found me at a meadow crossroads, beaks in the air, fanning
away the smell of a lumpy old bundle. Bury it, they said, and they paid me
three silver coins to do it. They looked very respectable. So I dug.

When I lifted the bundle, the string slipped and the cloth fell open. Jewels.
Gold necklaces. Piles of gold coins. I carried it home grinning so hard my
cheeks ached, and Mama Yeti waved the whole lane over to see.

Tashi Marmot saw it too, over his wall. Before sunset I was walking a long red
runner in the palace, hands together, while Snow Leopard Queen leaned forward
on her throne and asked if I had taken her treasure. A cloud covered the sun.
My knees had opinions.

Then I remembered the yak. So I told her everything. The magpie, the peacocks,
the spade, the smell.

> The cheap advice found me the gold. The honest advice was the only thing that let me lift my chin.

Her stern eyes softened, and she forgave me. At home, Tashi Marmot was waiting
by his gate with his ears flat, saying sorry. I held out my hand and asked him
to supper.

## The Yeti Way

**An honest heart is the greatest treasure.**

I paid a whole coin to be told to do as I was told. That is not wisdom. That is
how a Yeti ends up burying smelly bundles for strangers. It worked out by luck,
and luck is not a plan.

The yak's coin did not make me rich. It made me easy to believe. I stood in
front of a Queen with nothing to hide and nothing to keep straight in my head.

And once I had been forgiven, it seemed silly to hold anything against Tashi
Marmot. Babu Yeti handed him a full bowl, and we sat on the steps until the
stars came out.

Tell the truth first. Then pass the rice. ❄️❤️
