The wind was doing the thing it does in Magh, coming down off the ridge and
testing every gap in the cave door. Inside, under a blanket heavy enough to stop
a small avalanche, it should have been the warmest place on the mountain.

I was awake. Not for any good reason. My phone was in my paw and my face was blue
in a way that had nothing to do with my fur.

Next to me, Mama Yeti shivered.

## The click

I want to be honest about how long that took to register. It was not immediate.
She rustled. She turned. She pulled the blanket up around her ears. And I kept
tapping, because the thing I was reading was almost finished, and the thing I was
reading was not important.

Then she shivered again and made a small sound. Not a complaint. Yetis from her
side of the valley do not complain about cold. That is exactly why I heard it.

I locked the phone. One click, and the whole room went dark and honest.

That click is a small sound. At that hour, in that quiet, it is also a
confession.

## What the dark showed me

Here is what I had not noticed in twenty minutes of being right there beside her.
The cold was not the problem. The blanket was on my side. All of it. I had been
slowly dragging the warm off my wife while staring at a screen and thinking of
myself as a Yeti who takes care of his family.

So I fixed what I could reach. Blanket back over both of us, tucked at her
shoulder. The little star lamp on the nightstand switched on, that soft amber it
makes, the one that turns a cave into a den. Phone face down and done for the
night. The room went from blue light to gold light, and so did she.

> A cold Yeti in a warm bed is not a weather problem. It is a paying attention
> problem.

Then I pulled her in, and she came the way she always does, no ceremony, just fur
against fur. She let out the smallest sigh. The wind outside stopped being
anything I had to think about.

Twelve seconds of work. Twenty minutes of not doing it.

## The Yeti Way

**Warmth is not something you have. It is something you keep handing over.**

I had the blanket. I had the good spot. I even had the honest intention of being
a good husband, sitting right there in my chest where nobody could see it. None
of that reached her. The blanket did.

A screen is very good at making twenty minutes feel like one. That is its whole
job. Nobody in this family is going to be ruined by a phone. But the person
beside you gets cold in real time while you are somewhere else, and she will
almost never tell you.

So look over. That is the entire skill. Look over before she has to ask.

Put the phone down. Share the blanket. ❄️❤️
