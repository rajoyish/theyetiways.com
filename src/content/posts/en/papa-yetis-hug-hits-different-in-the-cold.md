The fire was behind us, which is the wrong side for a fire. I could hear it
doing its thing, that soft crackle like someone eating dry leaves very
politely, but all the warm was landing on our backs and none of it on our
fronts. Outside the cave mouth, snow sat on every rock like it had paid for
the seat.

I was smiling. I want that on the record. I had been smiling for a while,
because Papa Yeti was next to me and the fire was going and that is usually
enough.

Then the cold found the gap in my fur. There is always one gap.

## The plan was to say nothing

Here is the thing about being small and mint green next to somebody big and
blue. You do not want to be the one who is cold. Papa Yeti is never cold. He
walks into a blizzard the way Mama Yeti walks into the kitchen.

So I crossed my arms. Casually. The way you cross your arms when you are
thinking about something important and not at all because your teeth have
started a small argument with each other.

The shiver went through anyway. Top of the tuft to the toes and back. My smile
slid off one side of my face and I could not get it to climb back up.

I did not look at him. I looked at the snow. The snow was very interesting.

And then I felt him turn. Not the whole body, just the head. That slow look he
does, eyebrows down, where he is not asking a question. He is already
answering it.

## The heaviest arm on the mountain

He did not say a word. Papa Yeti says a lot of words, normally. He has words
about the weather, words about the yak, words about how I hold a spoon.

This time, nothing. He just shuffled over. The rock scraped under him, the fur
on his side brushed the fur on mine with a sound like someone folding a very
large blanket, and then his arm came down around me.

I need you to understand the weight of that arm. It is not a light arm. It is
an arm that has carried firewood up the whole ridge in one trip. When it lands
on you, it is less a hug and more a decision that has been made on your
behalf.

The shiver stopped. Not slowly. It just gave up, the way a snowball gives up
when you set it by the fire.

> I had been hiding the cold from him. He had already found it.

I closed my eyes. I leaned in until the blue went all the way around the
green. Somewhere the fire kept crackling and somewhere the wind kept going,
and none of it was my problem anymore.

## The Yeti Way

**You do not have to ask to be noticed. That is what a family is for.**

I spent the first half of that campfire trying to be a bigger Yeti than I am.
Arms crossed. Face pointed at the snow. Very busy being fine. Papa Yeti did
not argue with any of it. He did not tell me I was shivering. He did not ask
if I was cold. He would have got a lie, and he knew it.

He just moved the warm to where the cold was.

Grown-ups think this lesson is for kids, and it is, a little. But I watched
him do it, so I know the rest of it now too. When someone you love is
pretending to be fine, you do not have to win the argument. You can just
shuffle closer.

Cross your arms if you want. The arm is coming anyway. ❄️❤️
