The valley below our mountain does not sleep at night. It turns blue. Towering
mushrooms rise out of the moss like velvet umbrellas, and golden spores drift
past your nose like sparks from a chimney.

Nobody told me what sat at the top. On the tallest mushroom cap, a single golden
lantern fruit hovered in the cold air, turning quietly in place. It cast warm
rings across the dark bark. My ears warmed up first, then my tuft twitched. When
something glows like that, you do not stand on the ground watching.

## A spark in the dark

I scrambled up the spongy trunk, dug my paws into the cool moss, and hoisted my
round tummy over the rim. The golden lantern fruit floated right at eye level,
humming softly like bees asleep in a jar.

I reached out with both paws. The rind was warm, like a stone baked in the sun.
Mama Yeti always says *even the smallest heart carries enough warmth to
illuminate the deepest forest.* Holding that warm fruit against my mint fur, I
saw what she meant. You do not have to be tall to carry light.

I placed the fruit into a cradle of curling vines. Papa Yeti has a saying.
*When you nurture a single spark of wonder, the whole world blooms around you.*
The moment the fruit settled, honey-colored light rippled across the valley
floor. Thousands of violet and cyan blossoms opened at once, turning the canyon
into a sea of stars.

## Over the mist

Then an azure crystal bird darted through the branches above me. I sprinted
across the spongy cap and leaped straight into the misty chasm.

Air is thin when you have nothing under your paws. I caught a thick luminous
vine on the way down, looped around the bough until my tummy flipped, and shot
straight up through the spore clouds. Up near the canopy, a heavy branch loaded
with golden fruit was cracking. I wedged my shoulders underneath and shoved until
the joint clicked into its notch.

I patted the bark, proud of myself, until a startled chirp cut through the quiet.
A tiny forest sprite had lost its footing on a creeper, tumbling headfirst toward
the rocks. I kicked off the ledge, flipped through the mist, and cupped both paws
around the falling creature before sliding onto a purple mushroom cap.

> True courage isn't just about taking the leap. It is about having the gentleness to catch whatever is falling.

The sprite sat in my open palms, glowing cyan against my mint fur. I tucked it
against my round tummy and sat on the soft cap while the valley breathed.

## The Yeti Way

**Taking the leap is only the loud part of being brave.**

Anyone can run fast and fling themselves across an empty gorge. Momentum carries
you across a chasm whether you are brave or just reckless.

The real test comes when something small slips from the branch. Big paws are not
made just for swinging on vines or shoving heavy wood into place. If they cannot
cup together softly enough to keep a tiny creature safe, the climb does not mean
much.

Strength makes the jump. Tenderness makes the landing.

Catch what falls. Let the wonder bloom. ❄️❤️
