Somebody left a sled on our mountain. Bright red, glossy, a rope loop at the
front, wedged in an ice ledge no higher than my shoulders. The reddest thing on
a white mountain.

I did not think. I grabbed the rope with both paws and tugged. Nothing. Tugged
again. The sled came free, I sat down hard in the powder, and it slid into my
lap like it had been waiting for me.

My fur puffed out all over. I hugged it. Then I looked up the slope for somebody
to show. A sled nobody sees is half a sled.

## The grin that gave me away

Mama Yeti and Papa Yeti came over the rise, side by side, both smiling. I jumped
up, held the sled out so the red side caught the light, and bounced, looking
from one to the other for the nod.

Then I did the thing. Hugged the sled to my chest, turned half away from them,
and grinned. Not a happy grin. A mine grin. The one that says the paperwork is
done.

Mama Yeti's smile went soft. She tilted her head, raised one open paw, and shook
it slowly from side to side. My feet stopped bouncing. I had not told them
to.

## Charades on a cold slope

Nobody said a word. I can argue with words.

Mama Yeti knelt to my height. Eyebrows up, one paw open to the sky: a
question. Then she swept it across the whole empty valley below us. No Yeti. No
footprint.

I looked at the valley, then at my toes. One of them drew a small line in the
snow without asking me. I shook my head. Slowly. The reluctant kind.

Papa Yeti's paw landed on my shoulder. He crouched and mimed. He pointed at
me. Cheek on his paw, eyes closed: sleeping. Eyes open, cradling something
small and precious between his paws. Then he turned one paw over. Empty.
Eyebrows up.

Mama Yeti says *true kindness begins when we pause to consider what it feels
like to stand in someone else's shoes.* Yetis do not wear shoes. I got it
anyway. Some kid, somewhere, going to sleep tonight without their red sled. My
eyes went hot. I nodded, looked at the sled in my other paw, and it was a
different sled.

> The sled had not changed. I had just stopped being the only one in the story.

Mama Yeti pulled me into her fur. Papa Yeti nodded once. I gripped the rope.
My turn.

## The Yeti Way

**Found is not the same as yours.**

We walked down to the lower trail, the runners hissing behind me. There is a
tall wooden signpost by the path everybody uses. I stood the sled against it,
red side out, straightened the rope loop, and patted it twice. Then I stepped
back and my face did a proud smile before I told it to.

Papa Yeti ruffled my tuft. Mama Yeti took my paw. We walked home with nothing in
my other paw, and I pressed it flat to my chest and found something warm there.
Papa Yeti says *the things we let go of with love leave more room in our hearts
for a warmth that never fades.* I checked. There was room.

Somebody was going to see that sled by the signpost and run. I kept that
picture. *True joy isn't found in what we keep for ourselves, but in the quiet
peace of doing what is right.* Mama Yeti says that one. I would have said the sled. I would have been wrong.

Give it back. Keep the warm. ❄️❤️
