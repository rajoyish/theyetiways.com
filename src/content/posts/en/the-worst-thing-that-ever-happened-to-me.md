The apple was mine. I had carried it all the way from the shelf under the
lantern to the middle of the cave floor, which is a long way when you are
holding something round with two paws that do not fully agree with each other.

I had a little tune going. That is the part I think about now. I was humming,
and then the apple was on the floor.

## The thud

It did not even make a big noise. One flat thud, and then the sound of a round
thing rolling away from me across cold stone, getting quieter, which is somehow
worse than the thud.

The tune stopped. My face did the thing where it starts out as a normal face and
then folds in the middle without asking me first.

I want it on record that I understood the situation. It was an apple. There are
other apples. I knew that the whole time.

I cried anyway.

## What I was braced for

Then, footsteps. The padded kind, two sets, coming from both sides at once.

Papa Yeti came in from the left and Mama Yeti from the right, and they both had
their eyebrows down in the way grown-ups get when a thing is not funny to them
yet.

Here is what I expected. The speech. The one about how it is only an apple, and
how we do not cry about apples in this cave, and how I should go pick it up and
get on with my day.

They knelt down instead. Mama Yeti wiped my cheek with one thumb and said
nothing at all about my face being wet, which was the kindest part of it.

Then she brought her other paw around from behind her back.

The apple in it was enormous. Red like the sun going down behind the ridge.
Twice the apple I had lost, and I had lost a perfectly good apple.

> Nobody told me my sadness was the wrong size. They just quietly made the apple
> bigger.

I grabbed it with both paws and got it into my mouth before anyone could change
their mind. Then the fur closed in from either side, blue on one flank and pink
on the other, and I ate the rest of it inside a wall of family.

## The Yeti Way

**A small sadness handled fast is worth more than a correct lecture about how
small it was.**

They had the facts. It was fruit, I am a Yeti of the mountains, and the cave
floor is swept every morning. Being right was sitting there for the taking.

Being right would have taken longer. And what I would have learned is that when
something drops, I should go be sad about it somewhere out of the way.

What I learned instead is that in this cave, when a thing falls, two people
come. That one is going to matter later, when what drops is bigger than fruit.

I still eat apples with both paws. ❄️❤️
