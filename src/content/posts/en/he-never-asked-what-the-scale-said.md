The scale lives in the corner of the sleeping cave, under the window where the
snow light comes in blue and honest. Nobody remembers carrying it up here. It
has been in this family longer than Babu Yeti has.

I stepped on it before the morning had properly started. The dial did its little
shiver and settled, and I stood there looking down at it, very still, the way you
stand when you are hoping the mountain will change its mind.

Then I sighed. Not a small one. The kind you feel in your ribs on the way out.

## Then he came in carrying fruit

Papa Yeti padded in on those quiet feet, and he had the big wooden bowl with
him, the rough one, filled past the rim. Apples, plums, oranges, a mango sitting
on top like it had won something.

He set it on the table by the window. Softly. The way you put a thing down when
you do not want it to sound like a point being made.

He did not look at the scale. He did not look at me looking at the scale. He
nudged the bowl a little to the left, which is what he does with his paws when
his mouth has decided to stay out of it.

## What he did not say

Here is what he had available to him and did not use. That the dial was wrong.
That I looked wonderful. That I should not worry about it, said in the voice
people use right before they explain your own feelings back to you.

Every one of those is an argument. And an argument would have meant the number
was worth arguing with.

So instead there was fruit on the table, in the morning light, carried up the
mountain by a Yeti who had gone and gotten it without being asked.

I looked up at him. The gloom did not lift so much as get crowded out.

> He never argued with the number. He put something good next to it and let me
> reach.

So I reached. I took the reddest apple in the bowl and bit into it, and the
crunch went all the way around the cave and came back. He grinned at me with
every square tooth he owns.

## The Yeti Way

**You cannot talk somebody out of how they feel about themselves, but you can
set something kind within reach and let them get there on their own.**

My husband has never once won an argument about how I look, because he has never
once started one. He goes down the mountain instead. He comes back with a bowl.

That is the whole trick, and it is not really a trick. Care that shows up as an
object on a table is care you can pick up. Care delivered as a speech is
something you have to stand there and receive.

There is still fruit by the window. He refills it and says nothing about it.

Go get the apple. Leave the dial alone. ❄️❤️
