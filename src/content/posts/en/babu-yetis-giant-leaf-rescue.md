The morning air in the Himalayan bamboo grove was wet and sharp. High above,
the mist rolled off the ridge, smelling of damp bark, green shoots, and cold
mountain rock.

We were walking the cliff path. Papa Yeti strolled on the left, humming into his
blue beard. Mama Yeti was on the right, her pink shoulders easy in the early
light. Our young apprentice red panda trotted beside my paws, ringed tail held
high. Everything was quiet until a scuffle rattled the canopy.

## The leap into the mist

A striped squirrel had been sleeping on a mossy branch when a snap of wind woke
it with a gasp. It scrambled down the bark in sheer panic, tiny claws skittering
across the stones, and bolted right past our feet.

Before Papa Yeti could stop humming, the squirrel ran up a slanted bamboo stalk
near the cliff edge. The stalk bent like a bow and flicked the creature clean
into the sky.

There was nothing beneath it but open air and a deep ravine swallowed in cold
fog.

The squirrel plummeted straight down into the chasm, limbs flailing in the
mist. Papa Yeti and Mama Yeti rushed to the rim, eyes wide in alarm. The
apprentice red panda whined from the ledge, pressing against my knee.

## The leaf in the updraft

Fear tried to freeze my paws. But Mama Yeti always told me that in life's
deepest ravines, hope can rise on the gentlest breeze: it only takes one willing
heart to guide it.

Right at the rim, clinging to damp stone, grew a giant mountain leaf. It was
wide as a sled, trembling in a warm draft rising from the valley. I leaned over
the drop and clapped my green paws together at chest height with everything I
had.

The morning sun broke through the clouds, slicing golden light across the grey
mist. The rising air caught the broad leaf and lifted it like a kite.

The leaf banked across the chasm and swooped beneath the falling squirrel. The
tiny creature hit the wide green cushion with a soft thud, completely safe.

With another swell of the breeze, the leaf sailed over the cliff edge. It
settled gently onto a sturdy mossy branch beside our path. Papa Yeti cradled the
exhausted squirrel into the moss with his blue paws, while Mama Yeti stroked its
fur until it curled into a peaceful ball.

> True strength isn't about standing above others; it’s about reaching out to catch them when they fall.

## The Yeti Way

**You are never too small to make a giant difference when you lead with courage and kindness.**

When that squirrel fell, Papa Yeti was twice my height and Mama Yeti was twice
my width, but neither was standing by the leaf. I had no wings or magic. I only
had two small paws, a mountain breeze, and a refusal to let someone vanish into
the dark.

Life rarely asks you to move a mountain. It asks you to notice what is growing
at the edge, listen for the rising breeze, and act before fear tells you to turn
away.

Watch out for the little ones on the path, trust the wind, and always reach out
to catch what falls. ❄️❤️
