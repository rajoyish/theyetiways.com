The morning sun broke through the Himalayan forest canopy, turning the moss on
the fallen logs into bright velvet. The air smelled of damp earth and pine
needles.

I crouched on a fallen trunk with a slender bamboo stick, lining up a strike
against a brown walnut. One gentle tap, just to see how far it would roll.

## The golden whirlwind

Then distant gasps broke my focus.

Down the trail, two children were frozen in fear. A boy in a green tunic and a
girl in a yellow dress crouched low as a dark swarm of bees poured out of the
high branches.

You do not measure your paws when someone small needs help.

I dropped the walnut, gripped my stick, and leapt from the log. My paws hit the
dirt and I sprinted down the path in a kung fu dash. The buzzing grew into a
roar.

I vaulted right between the children and the swarm.

With a deep breath, I threw myself into a whirlwind spin. The bamboo stick sliced
through the air with a clean swoosh. Papa Yeti always taught me that with gentle
intent and a brave heart, even the fiercest storm can be turned into golden
light.

The rushing air whipped up pollen from the brush. The swarm did not strike. It
dissolved into a sparkling cloud of golden dust, scattering harmlessly across
the sunbeams.

## Flat against the bark

The spin was heroic. The stop was not.

The whirlwind carried far more momentum than my chubby legs could steer. Before
I could plant my feet, the spin launched me sideways.

Thud.

I hit a rough tree trunk flat against the bark, arms and legs splayed out. Bits
of dry moss drifted down while I hung there, stuck fast to the pine sap.

> True heroes aren't measured by whether they stumble. They're measured by the courage that made them leap in the first place.

I unpeeled my belly from the sticky bark with a soft plop and dropped to the
grass.

My head spun, but I rubbed my forehead with one green paw. Even when life knocks
you off balance and leaves you stuck, brush off the dust, find your footing, and
stand proud.

The children peeked up, safe and unharmed. I grabbed my stick, gave it a twirl,
and struck a proud hero stance. The boy and the girl stepped forward, smiling
with relief, and bowed in gratitude.

## The Yeti Way

**Courage doesn't depend on your size; it depends on the strength of your heart to stand between danger and those in need.**

When that dark swarm swept down, I did not ask if my bamboo stick was thick
enough. I only saw two children who needed a shield. The leap comes from love,
not from a promise that your landing will look graceful.

You might spin so hard to protect someone that you end up plastered to a pine
trunk with moss in your fur. What counts is that you unstick yourself, shake off
the dust, and smile back at the ones you sheltered.

Stand between the storm and the ones in need, hold your ground, and never fear a
messy landing. ❄️❤️
