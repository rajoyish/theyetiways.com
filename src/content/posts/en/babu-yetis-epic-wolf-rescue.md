High in the pine above the path, the air smelled like cold needles and wild
apples. I had picked two from the bare shrub: one for my belly, and one to hold
while I chewed.

Down below, the quiet snow began to crunch. A woodcutter walked home with firewood
on his back, and riding the sticks was a ginger cat with white paws. The cat had
its tail wrapped over the man's neck like a scarf, purring loud enough to shake
the branches.

## The bravest orange puffball

Then the pale sun ducked behind a grey cloud, and three lean wolves stepped onto
the path. They spread into a slow half-circle, amber eyes locked on the
woodcutter.

The man stumbled into the deep drift and sat down hard, holding up one arm while
his breath puffed white.

That was when the cat jumped.

It did not run up a tree. It leapt off the firewood, landed between the woodcutter
and the wolves, arched its back, and puffed every orange hair until it looked like
a pinecone. Then it hissed so hard it sounded like green wood on a fire.

It was the size of one wolf paw, but it stood its ground.

## A drop from the pine

You cannot sit on a branch eating apples while a tiny stranger risks everything
for his human.

I bit into my apple, kicked off the bark, and dropped.

Falling through pine boughs is a blur of needles and snow. I hit the trail
between the cat and the wolves with a heavy thud, throwing up white powder. The
cloud broke, a golden shaft of sun pierced the mist, and the parting vapour
curled into a dragon edged in gold. Papa Yeti always says, *no matter how dark
the winter path, hold your ground. Light has a way of breaking through the clouds
just when you need it most.*

I stood tall, pushed my chest out, planted both green paws, and scowled.

The wolves squinted in the sudden sun, took one look at a mint-green baby Yeti
landing from the sky, and scrambled backward into the brush.

> True courage isn't measured by your size, but by the fierce willingness to
> stand between the ones you love and the shadows that surround them.

The woodcutter fell to his knees with tears of relief, scooping the cat against
his chest. The cat purred against his beard.

I scrambled back up my pine before anyone could ask questions. When the man stood
up, brushing the snow from his tunic, I leaned over my branch and dropped my
second apple straight into his mittens.

## The Yeti Way

**Courage is not the absence of fear, but the choice to stand in front of someone
who needs you.**

That cat had no business fighting wolves. It had no fangs or heavy claws. All it
had was an enormous heart and a refusal to let its friend face the cold alone.
When someone stands up for love with sheer grit, the only thing to do is jump
down and stand beside them.

We cannot stop wolves from showing up on the path. Cold winds blow, and sometimes
we trip into the drift. But the greatest battles are not won with fury, but with
a brave heart, unexpected allies, and the quiet warmth of a shared kindness.

Stand tall for the ones who cannot run, and always save an apple for the road.
