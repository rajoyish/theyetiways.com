Babu Yeti was sulking. Chin on both paws, lower lip out far enough to hang a
lantern on, eyes fixed on one square of stone floor as if it owed him money.

I do not know what the stone had done. I still do not. The hearth crackled, the
snow sat quiet in the cave mouth, and Babu Yeti was staring a hole through the
floor of my own den.

I planted my feet wide beside the fire, held out one paw, and wiggled my
fingers. Nothing. Not a flicker. A sulk that deep does not look up for fingers.

## The snowflakes nobody else could see

So I crossed one leg behind the other, the way a Yeti stands when he is about
to do something a grown Yeti should not, and I started swatting at snowflakes.

There were no snowflakes. That was the point. I swatted one with the left paw,
one with the right, tracked each invisible flake across the cave with my whole
face, and let the tuft on my head bounce with every swing. Whoosh. Whoosh.

Between swats I glanced sideways at the green lump on the floor. Still chin on
paws. But the eyes had moved. They were following my paws now, and one corner
of his mouth had started to twitch, like a smile trying to sneak out without
permission.

That is the moment you commit. There is no dignity left to save.

## The wobble

I lifted one leg, spread both arms, and pretended to lose my balance. Big
circles. Enormous circles. A Yeti my size going over like a tree in a storm,
and then dropping into a crouch so my face landed level with his.

He giggled. A real one. Then he sat up and clapped.

The claps did it. I went again. Lunge left, balance, lunge right, a kind of
dance if you were feeling generous, and he bounced on his round bottom and
clapped in time, grinning so wide the tuft on his head quivered.

Then he stopped clapping, sat as tall as he could, and put both little paws up
in a copy of my pose. Wobbly. Serious. Perfect.

> I never asked what the stone had done to him. By then it did not matter to
> either of us.

I dropped into a lunge and opened my arms as wide as they go. He scrambled up,
took one eager step, and leapt the rest, and I folded him into my chest with my
cheek on his head. Then I stood and swayed, and from inside the hug one small
paw came out and swatted a snowflake above us. Just one. So I would know he
had seen them too.

## The Yeti Way

**You do not have to find out what is wrong before you can make it better.**

I used to think the job was to get to the bottom of the sulk. Ask. Wait. Ask
again. But a kid with his chin on his paws does not want a meeting. He wants
proof that the world outside the sulk is still worth looking up at.

So you become the most ridiculous thing in the cave. You swat at nothing. You
fall over on purpose. You let him watch you not care how you look, and you
keep going until the smile sneaks out. The hug does the rest.

Be silly enough, and the sad thing has to wait outside. ❄️❤️
