The wind was doing its low howl through the cave mouth, the one that sounds
like the mountain is bored. The moss on the walls had dimmed to its night glow,
and Babu Yeti was fighting the blankets.

Not crying. Just turning. Left, right, left, the woven blanket rustling like a
yak walking through dry grass. I sat on the edge of the stone bed and watched
him with the face I make when I am worried and trying to look calm. Nobody has
ever been put to sleep by that face. I keep making it anyway.

## Then Papa Yeti walked in with a rock

That is what I saw first. My husband, big and blue and moving very carefully,
carrying a rock in both paws like it might spill.

Then the rock started to glow. Warm and amber, the shade a fire goes when it is
nearly done. A soft chime came off it, so faint I thought I had imagined it,
and Papa Yeti began to hum. Low. Off-key. The same three notes he hums when he
is fixing something and does not want to be asked about it.

Babu Yeti stopped turning long enough to look. So did I.

## Where the magic actually was

I did not plan what happened next. I reached over and laid my paw across the
top of the crystal, the way you shade a candle from a draught.

The light went sideways, through my fingers, and up onto the ceiling. Shapes.
Slow, drifting, fuzzy-edged shapes, sliding across the rock like something
swimming under ice. Babu Yeti's eyes went wide, then went soft. A tiny cooing
sound came out of him, the one he has made since he was small enough to fit in
one paw.

The hum turned into a real lullaby somewhere in there. I do not know which of
us started it.

> The crystal was never the magic. The magic was two tired grown-ups pointing
> at the same ceiling.

By the time the last shape drifted off the wall, he was gone. Mouth open. One
paw flung out of the blanket. Breathing in that slow, deep rhythm that is the
best sound in the whole cave.

Papa Yeti and I stood there longer than we needed to. The far fire crackled.
Nobody said anything, because nothing we could say would have been better than
the breathing.

## The Yeti Way

**You cannot worry a child to sleep. You can only make the room stop asking
anything of him.**

I had been sitting at that bedside doing the hardest thing I know how to do,
which is caring loudly and silently at the same time. Babu Yeti felt every bit
of it. A worried grown-up at the edge of the bed is not a comfort. I was one
more thing in the room that is awake.

Papa Yeti brought the light. I brought the shadows. On our own, each of us would
have been a Yeti holding a rock. Together it was a sky.

That is most of parenting, I think. Not one big fix. Two small, tired paws
finding the same thing to do at the same moment, and Babu Yeti noticing that
the grown-ups have stopped bracing.

Dim the light. Hum off-key. Point at the ceiling together. ❄️❤️
