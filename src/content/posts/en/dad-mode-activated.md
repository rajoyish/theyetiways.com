There is a pine that leans out over the waterfall, and the branch that holds me
is the third one up. I have been testing that branch for years. It holds.

I had a dandelion with me. No reason. It was standing in the grass on the way
up, so I carried it the way you carry a thing you have no plan for. Sun through
the needles. Water going somewhere far below. I filled my cheeks and blew, the
seeds went spinning out over the drop, and I sat there grinning like a Yeti with
an empty morning.

That was the last easy breath in this story.

## The gold

Below me the river was doing what it always does, and Babu Yeti was in it.

He was fine. I want that on the record before I tell the rest. His fur holds him
up like a cork, both paws were in the air, and he wore the mildly interested
face of somebody riding a cart to market. He looked up at the pines to see if
anybody had noticed.

I do not remember deciding. I remember the bare stem leaving my paw and never
hearing it land. Something warm came up behind my eyes, the way a lantern comes
up when you turn the wick, and the branch whipped back hard enough to throw the
last seeds into the air.

Then the water. Paw, spray, paw, spray, the river holding me because I had not
given it permission to do anything else. *When love fuels your purpose, no
current is too strong and no leap is too far.* I say that in the cave, with tea
in my paw. It is much louder out on the water.

I got both arms under him. Warm, heavy, smelling like river. His arms locked
around my neck and we came up out of the mist together.

## The drop of water

I landed on my back. That was the one part of it I would call a decision.

> True strength is not just braving the fiercest storm, but being the soft
> ground where another lands safely.

Grass, a tumble, a long slide, arms and legs flat out like a starfish, and Babu
Yeti riding my chest with his eyes squeezed shut and then very open. He pushed
up onto his paws to see where we had ended up. Unhurt. Unimpressed.

So I stayed down. Smile coming back. Eyes drifting half shut.

Then a single drop of water fell off a pine needle and landed square on my
forehead, and I went off like a struck bell. Eyes wide. Gold all the way up.
Over one drop. It drained back out and I lay there laughing at myself, wet fur
in the grass, ready to take on a raincloud.

## The Yeti Way

**A father's alarm has no dial on it, and that is the arrangement, not the
fault.**

Babu Yeti settled across my chest, and one last seed off my own dandelion
drifted out of the sunlight and caught on his nose. He crossed his eyes at it.
He puffed one small breath and sent it back up. We both followed it.

*Even in the wildest rush of life, peace can return on the breath of a single
floating seed.* I did not have that line yet, lying there. I had the seed.

That is what the whole crossing paid. Not thanks. Not a story he will ever tell.
A seed, a puff, and a kid who was never worried, because somebody was on the
branch above him.

So blow the dandelion. Skip the wish. You are already standing in it. ❄️❤️
