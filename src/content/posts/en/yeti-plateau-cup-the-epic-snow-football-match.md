The pitch was snow, packed flat and lined in blue dye, so high on the plateau
that the peaks looked close enough to lick. Both stands were full of marmots,
pikas, and blue sheep. Half waved turquoise flags for us. Half waved saffron for
them.

Us was Papa Yeti, Mama Yeti, me, and Snow Leopard in goal. Them was Wild Yak,
Red Panda, Alpine Musk Deer, and a Polar Bear so wide he barely needed to move.
The monal referee set the ball on the centre spot like it was an egg.

## The one I missed

They scored first. Alpine Musk Deer hopped clean over Papa Yeti's leg and curled
one past Snow Leopard's paws, and the saffron stand went off like fireworks.

Then the ball came to me. A long roll from Snow Leopard, open snow, nobody
between me and the Polar Bear. I had the whole run to think about it, which was
the problem. I kicked. The ball went wide. Very wide.

The turquoise stand made a sound like a yak sitting down.

The whistle went for half-time, and I walked to the bench looking at my feet,
because my feet were the only ones who had seen it up close.

## The shortest speech in Yeti history

Mama Yeti knelt in the snow in front of me. She did not say it was fine. She did
not say next time. She cupped my cheek, took my hand, and squeezed it twice.
That was the whole speech.

Then Papa Yeti drew a plan in the snow with a stick: three dots and one long
curvy line to the goal. Snow Leopard studied it like serious tactics. We stacked
our hands. I wiped one eye and nodded like I understood the curvy line.

The second half went fast. Mama Yeti scored from the far side of the mountain.
Wild Yak answered like a small avalanche. Papa Yeti tied it. Snow Leopard caught
one a whisker from the net. And then Papa Yeti rolled the ball to me, the Polar
Bear came at me like a glacier, and I kicked it under his paw.

It rolled over the line slowly. Slowly enough for me to not believe it.

> The miss happened in front of the whole plateau. The goal happened because
> three of them kept passing me the ball anyway.

At the final whistle Wild Yak sat down in the snow, and Papa Yeti pulled him up
by the hoof. I shook Red Panda's paw. The cup was carved wood, and I held it
the whole way home.

## The Yeti Way

**Your nerve comes back when your family keeps passing you the ball.**

Nobody fixed my kick at half-time. Nobody explained football to me. I got a
squeeze, a stick drawing, and then, the very next chance, the ball again. If
they had stopped trusting me after the miss, I would have started believing the
miss.

That is the part grown-ups sometimes skip. The pep talk matters less than the
next pass. You can tell a kid you believe in him all afternoon. Or you can just
put the ball at his feet.

Also, Wild Yak is still our friend. Winning is better when the other team gets
up smiling.

Keep passing. ❄️❤️
