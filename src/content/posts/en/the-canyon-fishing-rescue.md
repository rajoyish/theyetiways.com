The moss on the canyon ledge was soft and warm from the golden-hour sun. Babu Yeti and I sat quietly, our long wooden fishing staff held steadily over the sparkling river below. We were enjoying the gentle canyon wind and the quiet peace.

A sweet elderly woman in a pink floral cardigan was walking slowly across the low wooden footbridge behind us. It was a perfectly peaceful afternoon, the kind where the world feels completely still.

## The broken bridge

Then came a sharp, violent crack of splintering wood. A sudden yelp echoed through the canyon, followed by the sound of rushing wind and a heavy splash. 

The plank had snapped right out from under her. She fell into the shallow river. Babu Yeti let out an anxious gasp, pointing frantically down into the swirling water. 

My heart dropped. There was no time to think. I gripped the wooden staff with both paws, braced my rounded feet heavily against the mossy ledge, and swung the heavy line deep into the sparkling water.

## The water dragon

The line went taut instantly, nearly pulling me over the edge. I pulled fiercely backward, straining against an immense weight. The water below began to swirl rapidly, kicking up a thick fog. I expected the heavy resistance of the current, but what erupted from the river was a colossal, serpentine water dragon.

Composed of sculpted ice and swirling currents, the majestic creature rose gracefully over the stone ledge. The canyon flooded with brilliant blue light. It gently held the surprised elderly woman on its broad snout and safely placed her onto the soft moss beside us. True magic doesn't always come from a wand; often, it rises from the darkest waters the moment we choose to help someone in need.

> "Sometimes, when the bridge beneath us breaks, the greatest miracle is the friend who dares to cast a line into the deep."

The magnificent dragon gave a compassionate smile before bursting into a beautiful shower of sparkling water droplets. As the woman bowed deeply in heartfelt gratitude, the staff suddenly bent sharply downward again. I pulled backward with a short grunt, reeling the line completely up. A giant, glowing blue ice-fish landed safely on the ledge, and Babu Yeti cheered in absolute triumph.

## The Yeti Way

**Cast your line with compassion, and you will reel in wonders far greater than you ever expected to catch.**

When we sit by the river, we usually only hope for a simple meal. We forget that the world is much deeper, and the currents carry more than just fish. The river knows when someone is in trouble, but it still needs hands to reach into the water. 

The next time you see someone fall, do not hesitate to act. Throw your line and give whatever strength you have in that moment. The magic of the canyon always responds to a generous heart.
