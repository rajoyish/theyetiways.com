Morning mist on the stone terrace. Pine boughs heavy with fresh powder. Cold air
biting the tip of my snout.

I took my stance like a blue boulder settling into bedrock. When a Yeti decides
to lead forty-two forms of Tai Chi, he brings dignity to the mountain. Sinks
into his heels, squares his shoulders, and prepares to be an ancient master
before breakfast.

## The leaf on his nose

Then the solo Erhu started down in the valley, thin and sweet as honey over cold
porridge.

I moved into the opening forms with severe focus. Weight left, paw warding off,
chest solid. Beside me, my wife did not look like she was executing an ancient
martial discipline. Mama Yeti looked like warm river water pouring around a
stone, floating through the single whip without putting any weight on her heels
at all.

Between us stood Babu Yeti. His eyebrows were clamped together in fierce
concentration. Every time I stepped into a bow stance, he took two bouncy hops
to keep up, his mint-green tuft trembling with effort. By the riverbank, a
golden birch leaf drifted down toward his snout. He did not break form. He just
crossed his eyes, blew the leaf into the stream, and delivered a small round
punch that would have startled an alpine beetle.

## Above the sea of clouds

By the high ridge, forty-two forms had stopped sounding poetic. On a narrow
shale ledge in thick blue fur, it is a very long conversation with your thigh
muscles.

I sank into Snake Creeps Down. My knee creaked like a frozen cedar branch. Rising
into the Golden Rooster, I balanced on one shaking leg, certain the routine
depended on me not tipping into the abyss.

Mama Yeti was already up, light as thistle on the wind. Beside her, Babu Yeti had
his knee tucked against his tummy, little green toes gripping the timber,
beaming at the clouds. He was not fighting the mountain. He was just standing
where it met his paws.

> You cannot force harmony with stiff shoulders; you have to let the rhythm
> carry you together.

At the summit, sunset turned the prayer ribbons to gold. We swept through the
lotus kick and drew our paws down for the closing form. Babu Yeti tapped his
heel, matched our breath, and let out a soft sigh that fluttered his chest fur.

## The Yeti Way

**Mastery is not being the heaviest rock on the mountain; it is learning to move
at the speed of the ones you love.**

I began that dawn thinking leadership was about iron discipline. I thought if I
held the postures hard enough, my family would fall into line behind me.

Instead, they showed me that moving together is not a solo drill with
spectators. Mama Yeti brought the grace that kept us fluid. Babu Yeti brought
the joy that kept us light. By sunset, my shoulders had dropped, and my paws were
following the music of our breath.

Drop the tension. Soften the knees. Move with your circle. ❄️❤️
