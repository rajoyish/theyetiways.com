Ten eggs sat on our mossy wall, swinging their red shoes. I waved. They waved
back. The egg at the end put rather a lot of himself into it. Behind us, the
snow still sat on the peaks, but the meadow was warm enough to tickle my toes.

He wobbled over the edge. I stopped waving. There was a soft thump in the moss,
a crack, and a yellow chick where an egg had been. I looked at the chick. Then
at the shell. I had a few questions, and the chick had already hopped away.

## A very busy repair crew

The castle door opened. Two woolly ponies trotted out with two felt guards
behind them. They had the sort of march that makes you think someone knows
what they are doing. I watched closely. So did Papa Yeti.

They pushed the halves together. The chick popped up through the gap. Well.
That would need some work.

Another egg fell, and the helpers brought a daisy chain. Then a leaf. One
chick wore a shell half like a cap while the guards saluted. I liked that plan
best, though it did leave half the egg missing. Papa Yeti's shoulders bounced
beside me. His hand felt warm on my fur.

Then came the honey. A guard got a shell stuck to his hand. He shook it, which
seemed a poor way to mend an egg, but a good way to make me clap. The ponies
tried yarn and tied up their own legs. Every new plan ended with a chick free
on the grass and the helpers looking at two bits of shell.

## Everyone had somewhere to go

The row grew shorter from one end. Mama Yeti knelt by the flowers with her
hands open, and the chicks gathered at her feet. Each time I looked over,
there was more yellow fluff down there and more empty wall up above.

> The chicks kept leaving before their repairs were finished.

At last there were no eggs left. I looked along the whole wall to be sure.
Nobody fell. The moss cushion was full of hopping, and the guards had run out
of eggs to mend. Their little castle door opened again anyway.

This time they came out empty-handed. The ponies led the chicks across the
meadow, and the guards marched with them. Papa Yeti and Mama Yeti stood beside
me as we waved. I could see everyone much better down on the grass.

## The Yeti Way

**Look at who has hatched before you try to put the shell back.**

I understood why the guards kept trying. We had started with a row of eggs,
and the pieces were right there. It looked like a job they ought to finish.
They even had horses to help, which is more help than I get with most things.

But Mama Yeti had her hands ready for the chicks. They could hop into her
palms or stay by her feet. Watching them, I stopped wishing the wall would
fill up again. There was room beside us for the whole flock.

Save me a place in the parade. ❄️❤️
