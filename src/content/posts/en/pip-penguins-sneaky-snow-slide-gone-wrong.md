The top of the hill was all honey light and fresh powder. The sun sat low over
the peaks, and the snow squeaked under every step like it was keeping a secret.

I dragged the old birch-bark tray up the crest by its rim. Nuri Bear bounced on
her hind legs beside me. Pip Penguin hopped in circles, peeping like a kettle.
I sat in front, Nuri Bear climbed in behind me with her paws on my shoulders,
and Pip Penguin took the back to give us a gentle push. We had a plan.

It was a good plan. It lasted about as long as plans do.

## The push nobody ordered

Pip Penguin let go of the rim.

He waddled back up the crest with a squint I should have recognised. Then he
flopped onto his belly and came tobogganing at us like a small black-and-white
torpedo. Thump. The tray tipped over the lip, and so did my stomach.

We were fast. Then we were spinning. Pines, peaks, pines again. Nuri Bear
squeezed her eyes shut and hugged my back. Behind us, Pip Penguin squawked like
someone who had planned the push and not one thing after it.

## The catch at the bottom

I looked over my shoulder. Pip Penguin was clinging to the rear rim with both
flippers, his pink feet kicking at the sky, and the rim was winning.

I did not decide anything. I twisted round, reached back with one hand, and
grabbed his flipper as it slipped. He was heavier than he looked. I pulled him
in beside me, and he wrapped himself around my arm like a scarf. Nuri Bear
hugged both of us from behind.

Then the snowdrift. Whumpf.

For one long white moment there was nothing but powder hanging gold in the air.
Then three heads popped out of the drift, blinking.

Pip Penguin sank until only his eyes showed, both flippers pressed over his
beak. Nuri Bear stared at him. So did I. Then I scooped up a handful of snow and
flicked it right at his face.

> I could have been angry at the bottom of the hill. I was too busy being glad he was still on the tray.

## The Yeti Way

**Catch your friend first. Work out whose fault it was later, or never.**

Pip Penguin caused the whole mess. He was also the one about to fall off. Both
things were true on that slope at once, and only one of them needed my hand.

Friends will push you over edges you were not ready for. Mostly they mean it as
a joke. When the joke goes wrong, the scolding can wait. The grip cannot.

By sunset we had dug the tray out and squeezed back onto it, and Pip Penguin was
pointing a flipper up the hill again. I let him. Some lessons need one more
ride. ❄️❤️
