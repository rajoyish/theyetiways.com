The forest below our mountain glows at night. Nobody told me this. I walked into
it and found out, which is how I find out most things.

Pink mushrooms taller than the cave. Blue trees that hum. Little lights with
somewhere to be. Behind all of it, bells.

Then I saw the vine. Thick, pink, lit from the inside, hanging there for one
Yeti of exactly my size. My tuft stood straight up.

## The letting go

I took it in both paws and ran. The vine creaked, took my weight, and swung me
out between the trees in one long arc. Papa Yeti stood on the mossy ridge to my
left, one paw up, head turning to follow me.

Papa Yeti has a saying. *Sometimes you have to let go of what holds you to
discover how beautifully you can fly.* I had heard it a hundred times. At the
top of the arc, where the vine stops to think, I found out he meant my hands.

I opened both paws.

A soft snap. Then quiet. I tipped backwards into open air, arms and legs out,
the whole canopy above me, and I laughed. I checked on Papa Yeti on the way
past. Both paws up. Eyebrows somewhere near his tuft.

Then his face changed. He saw I was laughing and grinned like somebody who had
just remembered whose kid I am.

## The way down

Falling is slower than you expect when nothing is in a hurry. I drifted under a
blue mushroom the size of a house. A bubble floated by. I touched it with one
claw and it burst into sparkles across my face. I gasped with no sound.

Then a beam of pink light angled down through the trees, and I landed on it,
stomach first, and went. Sparks behind me. Paws under my chin. He also says
*even a fall can become a magical slide if you look at the journey with joy and
wonder.* I had no time to look at anything. The slide happened anyway.

And below me, the whole way, soft thuds on the moss. Papa Yeti was running. Not
shouting. Not reaching. Head tilted up, keeping pace.

> He never once tried to grab me out of the air. He just made sure that wherever
> I came down, he would already be there.

The beam ended. I ran off it onto a pink mushroom cap that bowed all the way
down and threw me into a flip I had not planned. I landed on both feet on a
small hill of glowing moss. Arms up. Knees bent. Alive.

Papa Yeti bounded up, dropped to one knee, and held out one wide paw. I hit it
with both of mine. Then he scooped me onto his shoulder and the forest opened up
around us, pink and blue and endless.

## The Yeti Way

**Letting go is easy when you know who is running underneath.**

I thought the brave part was opening my paws at the top of the swing. It was
not. Opening your paws is one moment. Papa Yeti did the long part. He read my
face on the way past, decided I was fine, and spent the whole fall keeping up.

He says *no matter how high you leap or how far you tumble, the greatest joy is
having someone proud waiting to catch you.* He did not catch me in the air. He
caught me on the hill, on one knee, paw out. That is the kind he means.

I will let go of bigger things than a vine one day. When I do, I will listen for
the thuds.

Let go at the top. Someone is already running. ❄️❤️
