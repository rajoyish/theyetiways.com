I spent half the winter watching that white shape glide over the high
ridges, sure I knew what it was for. A creature that big, with scales like pearl
ice, belonged in our valley. After breakfast I thumped my ribs and marched up
the frozen path to claim a dragon.

Mama Yeti walked beside me with one raised eyebrow, her usual warning. Between
us, Babu Yeti toddled along the trampled snow, his single green curl bouncing in
the wind. Heavy crunching thuds for my boots, quick patters for his. Behind us,
our valley stayed quiet, right until the sky went dark.

## The shadow on the ledge

A cold shadow swept over the snow and my blue fur, dimming the morning sun. A
deep rush of air rolled down the rocks, and my boots stopped mid-stride.

It sat on the crag like a piece of the glacier that had learned to breathe.
When those pale wings unfolded, the sun lit up the translucent membranes with
gold. I took two stiff steps onto the ledge, reaching out with curled fingers to
claim it.

Mama Yeti stepped past and laid her warm paw over my curled fingers. That touch
snapped the pride out of my bones. Looking up at ancient turquoise eyes, I
realized my mistake. An open palm invites the extraordinary; a grasping fist
only chases it away. My knuckles uncoiled, flat and empty, offering peace.

## What Babu Yeti offered

Before I could wonder if a dragon cares about empty paws, Babu Yeti stepped
between us. He had no rope, no net, and no ambition to conquer anything. He
scooped up a handful of snow, patted it into a crumbly little ball, and held it
straight up at chest height.

The dragon lowered its snout over Babu Yeti. A slant of morning sun slipped
past the wing, and with one soft puff of cold nostrils, the snowball burst into
sparkling dust. Babu Yeti giggled as the glitter settled on his ears. I rested
my open paw against the cool scales of its jaw, and the beast folded its legs
into the snow, laying one enormous wing along the ledge like a ramp.

Mama Yeti held Babu Yeti against her chest, I steadied her climb, and together
we settled into the hollow behind that massive neck. When those wings beat, the
ledge fell away in swirling powder. We were guests of the sky, carried through
silver mist and out across a calm sea of clouds.

> You cannot own what belongs to the open sky, but with an open hand, you can earn its friendship.

## The Yeti Way

**When we let go of the need to conquer, we are finally free to soar.**

My first instinct was the oldest mistake a Yeti can make. I saw something
magnificent, and my hands wanted to close around it. Pride whispers that to love
something wonderful, you have to possess it or bring it home.

True connection never begins with a cage. Babu Yeti knew that without thinking:
you approach what is wild with joy, not a demand for obedience. By the time the
dragon glided down outside our ice home and circled into the evening sky, my
empty paws had held more wonder than any clenched fist ever could.

Open your hands to the wild world, and let the sky remain the sky. ❄️❤️
