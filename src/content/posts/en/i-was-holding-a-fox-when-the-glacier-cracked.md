The fox kit crossed the valley alone. I could tell from its prints, one wobbly
line of dots with nobody else's beside them.

So I sat in the snow hollow under the glacier, wrapped it in the grey blanket,
and held it until the shivering slowed. Mama Yeti was gathering pine branches.
Papa Yeti was rolling a snowball the size of Papa Yeti up to the old stone
pillar, for reasons he never explains.

## The ice cleared its throat

Then powder started landing on the blanket, sliding down the ice wall behind me.

I looked up. A thin crack was running across the blue ice high over my head, and
the glacier let out a long, low groan, like something very big waking up in a
bad mood. Nobody else looked up.

The crack went off like a frozen lake snapping in two, and a piece of glacier
the size of a house leaned out over the hollow.

Across the valley Mama Yeti dropped her branches and ran. Papa Yeti hoisted his
snowball, which should not be possible, and jumped onto the pillar with it. I
curled over the kit and made myself as round as I could.

Mama Yeti slid into the hollow on her knees and wrapped both arms around the kit
and me. Above us the slab tore free. I watched it come down until only a strip
of grey sky was left around it. Her arm did not move.

## One snowball

On the pillar Papa Yeti spun once and flung the snowball. So that was what it
was for.

It hit the slab dead centre with a boom that shook the pines. The whole thing
burst into glittering pieces that flew away from us and crumbled into dust. The
clouds parted, the sun poured through all that sparkle, and the kit wriggled out
to lick my cheek.

It was the most beautiful thing I have ever seen. I would like it never to
happen again.

> When towering walls of ice crumble into glittering dust, no hollow is deep
> enough to hide in. The collapse of our glaciers isn't a distant echo. It's a
> warning roar we must answer.

Papa Yeti bounded over in huge leaps, and the snow slid off the pillar behind
him like it had had enough too. We hugged in a heap. Then a yip came from the
pines, the kit's mother trotted out, and the kit hopped straight to her without
looking back. I was left holding a warm, empty blanket.

## The Yeti Way

**It takes a family's love to shield a single life, but it takes global courage
to save a world.**

The smallest among us can protect the most fragile life, but who will protect
their frozen home? That glacier did not crack because it was old. It cracked
because the mountains keep getting warmer, and ice that held for ages is letting
go.

The rivers that fill every pot of dal below this mountain start in that ice.
Papa Yeti had one snowball, and there will be more slabs. As ancient glaciers
shatter, we must stand together to shield those who cannot save themselves. That
takes a lot more Yetis than three.

Let's act now to cool our warming planet before the ice walls fall forever. ❄️❤️
