The sun was barely over the peaks, and the meadow was still wet with dew. I
closed the red door behind me very quietly and started down the flagstone path
with an empty bamboo basket hanging from one hand.

I was tired. Not the good kind, after a long day. The other kind, before the
day has even started. My shoulders were somewhere near my knees, and the basket
swung the way a basket swings when nobody has any plans for it.

## The door opens

Halfway down the path I let out a sigh so long the birds stopped to listen. I
did not look back at the cottage. If I looked back I would want to stay, and
the basket was not going to fill itself.

Behind me, the latch clicked. The door creaked. Then came footsteps, quick and
light on the stones, and the swish of somebody running through tall grass
without caring about the dew.

I turned around just in time to see my wife coming at me with her arms flung
wide open and the biggest smile on the mountain.

I did what any sensible Yeti does when he is about to be hugged at full speed.
I froze and gasped.

## The basket hits the stones

She hit me like warm weather. Both arms around me, her cheek pressed into my
shoulder fur, squeezing like she meant to keep me. I stood there with my mouth
open and the basket dangling off my fingertips.

Then I heard it bounce. A hollow wicker thump, a little roll, and silence. It
came to rest on its side in the yellow flowers, and I did not go after it.

She leaned back and looked at me. Something in my face gave up. I felt my
eyebrows come down and my eyelids come up, and I was smiling before I had
decided to. We touched noses. Then cheeks. Eyes closed, right there on the
path, with the lanterns still lit by the door.

> I had been carrying an empty basket. It turns out I had been carrying an empty face too.

The door swung open again and Babu Yeti tumbled out, giggling. He scooped up
the basket, ran it down to me hugged against his chest, and held it up with
both hands like a gift. I took the handle and put my other hand on his back.

## The Yeti Way

**A goodbye is the first thing you carry into the day, so let somebody make it
a good one.**

I was going to walk off without one. I thought I was being quiet and
considerate. Really I was just letting the tired decide how the morning went.

Mama Yeti did not fix the tired. The basket was still empty. The work was still
waiting. But I went down that path with a bounce I had not packed, and when I
looked back, there they both were on the step, waving.

Run out the door for the ones you love. They will carry it all day. ❄️❤️
