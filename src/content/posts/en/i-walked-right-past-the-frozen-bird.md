The storm had been on us since the light went flat. I was in front, which on a
day like that just means taking the wind so nobody behind me has to.

Babu Yeti was stepping into my footprints, which are spaced wrong for his legs.
Behind him came the snow hare and the red panda, wearing more snow than fur. My
whole job was the next ridge and a dry cave.

I went under the bare branch without looking up.

## Someone smaller than me was paying attention

A small paw caught my arm. Babu Yeti had stopped and was pointing straight up
with his whole arm, the way he points when he does not have the word yet.

There was a bird on that branch. Brown, round, frost crusted across its back,
eyes shut so hard the feathers bunched, shivering in tiny fast trembles. You
only see that if you are standing still.

I had walked under it. I was counting the ridge and the light and the cave. I
had not counted the bird.

I was not being cruel. I was being busy. On a cold enough day those look the
same from the outside, and they feel the same to whoever is freezing.

## What I did about it

So I stopped, pressed my paws together at my chest, and held them there until
the warm came up between them. It is not a trick. It is the thing I keep in
there anyway, let out on purpose.

The clearing went orange. The snow gave up in a widening ring and turned to wet
earth and moss, and the frost on that bird ran off in beads. It opened its
eyes, shook itself hard, put both wings all the way out, and chirped twice.

> Small acts of kindness carry the power to melt away heavy frosts, awaken
> dormant hope, and bring new life to tired spirits.

I picked a curled leaf off the thawed ground because I did not know what else
to do with my paws, and I blew on it, and while I was being foolish about a
leaf the bird came down and landed on my paw. I have hauled firewood up that
ridge in one trip and never held anything that carefully.

Then it hopped into Babu Yeti's cupped paws and he giggled with his eyes
squeezed shut, and the hare tucked against his side and the red panda put its
tail around the hare. Outside our ring the snow fell exactly as hard as before.

## The Yeti Way

**Even in the coldest storms, true warmth begins within your own heart and
radiates outward to shelter those who need it most.**

I did not make that warm out on the walk. It was in me the whole time, doing
nothing, while I spent it keeping precisely one Yeti comfortable. Me.

Babu Yeti was cold too. He was smaller and colder and he still looked up. A
storm does not make you blind. Being in a hurry does.

When we pause to care for the vulnerable around us, we create a sanctuary of
safety where everyone can finally rest and thrive. We never reached the cave
that night. We sat on wet moss in a circle of melted snow with a bird, a hare
and a red panda, and it was the warmest I have been all winter.

Look up under the branch. Something there is waiting for somebody to stop. ❄️❤️
