The gold came through the jagged part of the cave mouth first. Low, thin, the
kind of light the mountain only hands out before it changes its mind.

I ran for it. Real running, paws thudding on frost-covered stone, making a
high excited noise I would not want played back to me. I did not want to look at
that sky. I wanted to show it to somebody.

## He rolled over into the dark

Papa Yeti was a blue hill under the moss blanket. I nudged him. I nudged him
again, with more editorial opinion in it.

He groaned from somewhere under his ribs, the long groan of a Yeti who has
decided the day can begin without his supervision, and turned the other way. The
moss rustled once. The blanket came up over his shoulder like a door being
pulled shut.

So I stood in our own cave holding the best sky of the season and nobody to
point at it.

## What I took out to the threshold

I padded to the entrance slowly, and I want to be honest about the slowly. Some
of it was disappointment. Some of it was hoping he would hear the disappointment.

Then the mountains took my attention away from my own feelings, which they are
very good at. The peaks went from grey to rose to a gold so bright it hurt, one
ridge lighting after another like somebody walking a lantern down a hall. The
wind came up over the ice and went straight through my fur.

I was already doing the small cold arithmetic. He does not care about this. I
care about this. Those two facts sat down next to each other and made themselves
comfortable.

Then two heavy arms came around me from behind and pulled me back into all that
warm blue fur.

> He did not get up for the sunrise. He got up because I was watching it alone.

He did not explain himself. He put his chin on my head, which is his whole
vocabulary for this sort of thing, and we stood at the mouth of the cave and let
the sun finish climbing.

## The Yeti Way

**Being turned down is not the same as being left alone.**

He wanted the sleep. He genuinely did, and he is allowed to. What he did not
want was me out at the cold edge of the cave by myself with something beautiful
and nobody to hand it to.

That is the marriage, most days. Not matching each other beat for beat, because
we never have. It is one of us getting up anyway, blanket abandoned, and closing
the distance without making a speech about it.

I still think he owed me a look at that sky. I will take the arms instead.

Come find her at the cave mouth. Every time. ❄️❤️
