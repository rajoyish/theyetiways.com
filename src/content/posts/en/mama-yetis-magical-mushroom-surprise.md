The mist on that side of the ridge sits low in the moss and makes everything
further away than it is. I was behind the arched trunk with a paw on the wet
bark, not planning to be anywhere in particular.

A girl knelt in the fallen leaves with a dried gourd and poured water onto a
big green leaf for a spotted deer. Then she set her mushroom down beside it.
One mushroom. The last one, which I know because I watched her check the basket
afterward.

## The patch that gave her nothing

The deer ate it. She smiled the way you smile when you have handed over your
own dinner and decided that was fine.

Then she was up and running, the deer bounding beside her under the arch, both
of them kicking dry leaves everywhere. She dropped to her knees just past the
trunk and swept a patch of dark earth clear with both hands.

Bare soil. The colour of wet ash, and nothing in it.

She looked at the patch. She looked at the basket. The creak that basket made
when she set it down empty was the loneliest sound in that forest.

Babu Yeti had crept along the mossy base of the arch to keep watching her, and
his whole small face folded up. Then he turned around and came to get me. He
had a paw in each of mine before I understood what he wanted, tugging, pointing
across at her.

## What the light did

So I walked out into the open. The girl watched a pink Yeti come out of the
mist toward her and, to her enormous credit, gasped only once.

I knelt at the cleared patch and held one paw open above the soil. The light
between my paw and the earth went gold. It does that sometimes. Mushrooms came
up through the dark ground and swelled to full size, brown caps crowding the
whole patch, popping softly as they broke the surface.

> A single act of kindness can turn bare earth into a harvest of miracles.

She picked with both hands at once. Papa Yeti came over grinning and dropped a
whole pawful in her basket like he had grown them himself. Babu Yeti set one
mushroom on a green leaf for the deer and bounced on his toes the entire time
it ate.

By the time she stood up under the arch, the basket was overflowing.

## The Yeti Way

**When you share the last of what you have, the world has a magical way of
filling your basket.**

I did not do it because she was hungry. Plenty of hungry people cross that
forest and the soil stays soil. I did it because she poured water for an animal
that could not thank her, on a morning when she had nothing spare to pour.

And I would not have seen any of it without Babu Yeti hauling on my paw. The
gold was mine. The noticing was his, and the noticing is the harder half.

Then she took one mushroom off the top of that full basket and held it out to
him. Of course she did.

True abundance is not what we gather alone, but what we harvest together.

Share the last one. Stay and watch what comes up. ❄️❤️
