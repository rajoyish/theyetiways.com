Dinner smelled like it had been happening for a while before anybody called me.
Mama Yeti slid the plate across the table with one paw, that last little push so
it landed exactly where my paws already were. Ceramic on wood. Best sound in the
cave.

Then I looked down at what had arrived.

A full-grown pile of food. And lying beside it, a fork built for somebody with
twice my reach and none of my problems.

## The fork situation

I picked it up. I want that noted. Nobody handed it to me and nobody asked if I
could manage it, so I got both paws around the handle and lifted, and the thing
came up off the table at an angle I had not chosen.

It clanked. Into the plate, off the rim, back down.

I made a noise. Not a word. More the strained sound a small green Yeti makes
when the dinner is winning.

Here is what I was actually doing up there, and I am only admitting it because
it is over. I was deciding whether to quit on the whole meal and pretend I had
not been hungry in the first place. I was going to sit in front of a hot plate
and act full.

Then a big blue paw came down over mine.

## What he did not do

Papa Yeti did not take the plate away. That is the part I keep turning over.

He did not slide it back to Mama Yeti. He did not say the sentence I was braced
for, which is *let me do that for you*. He did not laugh, and he could have. He
put his paw over the fork, took it, and started cutting.

Small pieces. Then smaller ones. Steady and unbothered, like this was a job on
his list and not an emergency on mine.

> He did not fix me. He fixed the size of the thing in front of me.

When he was done he set the little wooden spoon in my paw. My spoon. The one
that fits.

I got a piece up on the first try. My eyes closed without asking me. The food
was still hot, which tells you he did not linger over it, and it was still my
dinner, which tells you the rest.

## The Yeti Way

**Helping somebody is not doing it for them. It is cutting the thing down to
their size and handing it back.**

I was too small for that fork. I am going to go on being too small for things
for a while yet, and I have made my peace with roughly none of it. What got me
through dinner was not somebody being stronger than the cutlery on my behalf. It
was somebody spotting the mismatch and quietly closing it.

The plate stayed mine. The bites got smaller. Nobody said a word about it, and
that was the kindest part, because a kid who gets rescued out loud learns that
he needed rescuing.

So if there is a small Yeti at your table losing a fight with the silverware,
you do not have to announce anything. Cut it up. Hand over the right spoon. Go
back to your own dinner.

I still ate it myself. ❄️❤️
