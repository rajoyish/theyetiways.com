The moss nest comes up to my chin and Mama Yeti smooths it flat across my chest
like she is closing a letter. The fire pit pops. Snow goes past the cave mouth
in no hurry at all.

Papa Yeti holds one flat paw over me without touching me. He does that. He says
he is checking the warm.

I was gone before he put the paw down.

## The bridge in my head

The valley in my head is purple and blue and lit by a moon that is much too big.
There is a low wooden bridge over a hollow of glowing silver mist, and my
parents are standing on it.

Then the mist stands up. Wolf-shaped, grey, no teeth and no claws, just pale
blue eyes coming over the rope rails, in front of them and behind.

Papa Yeti sweeps a leg flat along the planks. Whump. Two of them burst into
glowing snow. Mama Yeti pushes one open palm out at chest height and a third one
goes off like a sneeze in a flour sack.

They back into each other, shoulder to shoulder, each one facing a different
crowd. His palm goes out. Her foot goes out. Snow everywhere.

Mama Yeti has a saying, and my sleeping head handed it back to me right there.
*When the mist rises and the shadows close in, stand back to back with the ones
you love. Together, every fear shatters into glowing snow.*

## The bridge that did not hold

He cupped his paws. She stepped in. He threw her, and my dream lost her at the
top and found her again on the far bank, standing up out of her own landing.

That left Papa Yeti alone at the anchor post with the whole crowd on it. So he
put both palms on the post and pushed.

The rope slipped. The bridge tilted. Every spirit on it went down into the mist
with the planks, and so did he, and he landed in the deep powder as a perfectly
round blue ball with a face.

> He did not beat them. He took the floor away and went down with it, which is a
> thing you can only do when somebody is already standing on the other side.

Mama Yeti came down the bank, took his paw and pulled, and they stood in the
mist grinning at each other with snow still stuck in his fur. Then they bounded
up the slope paw in paw while the last planks fell behind them.

*Even when the bridge under you gives way, trust the hand that pulls you up, and
bound toward the crest together.* That one is his. On the crest they turned and
pressed a fist into an open palm, and the moon made a whole thing of it.

## The Yeti Way

**You can lose a whole bridge in the night and still wake up in the warmest
place on the mountain.**

I threw one punch in my sleep. I am told it was small and landed on nothing.
Whatever I was doing in there, the two of them did the actual work.

When I opened my eyes the valley was gone and so was the moon. Papa Yeti was
curled on my left with his arm across my tummy. Mama Yeti was on my right and I
was already leaning into her. The fire was down to orange. Snow still going past
the cave mouth, still in no hurry.

The bravest battles happen in our dreams. The real peace is the warmth of the
ones who watch over our rest.

Go fight your valley. They will be here. ❄️❤️
