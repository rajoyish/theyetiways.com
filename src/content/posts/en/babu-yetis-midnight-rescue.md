The moon was so bright that night you could count the pines by their shadows.
Papa Yeti and I sat at the front of our ledge, on the thick lip of snow that
hangs over the trail. Snowy Owl sat in the crooked pine behind me, pretending
not to care about anything.

Far below, a lantern came swinging along the trail. The old herb-gatherer from
the cabin down the valley was carrying it. He held a little indigo pouch to his
chest, the way you hold something you cannot replace. I pointed. Nobody walks
that fast in the snow at night for fun.

## Three pairs of yellow eyes

A cloud slid over the moon. Between the dark pines, three pairs of yellow eyes
blinked on, one after another, like somebody lighting lamps.

Three grey wolves stepped onto the trail. Heads low. Then they ran.

The old man ran too, lantern flying, looking back over his shoulder. Something
small and indigo slipped out of his coat and dropped into the snow. He did not
notice. You don't, when wolves are behind you.

I leaned so far over the lip that I heard myself gasp. The pouch was lying
right there on the trail, and the wolves were coming straight for it.

## The lip comes down

I pushed both hands deep into the snow lip. I have been told many times not to
jump on it, because it is heavy and it is not attached to anything much.

That was the whole idea.

I looked at Papa Yeti. I did not say anything. I did not have to. He planted
his hands beside mine, the moon came out hard and silver, and we pushed.
Something under us creaked. A crack ran the length of the ledge, and the lip
went down in a white roar, right in front of the wolves.

When the snow dust settled, there was a wall across the trail taller than all
three of them. They skidded, ears flat, tails low, and decided they had
somewhere else to be.

I scrambled down and dug out the pouch. Snowy Owl dropped out of the sky,
grabbed the red cord in his feet, and was gone toward the cabin lights before I
could explain the plan. Owls never wait for the plan.

By the time I reached the cabin window, the old man had the pouch in his hands
and the herbs in a clay cup. A young mother held it to the lips of a little girl
buried in a patchwork quilt. She sipped. Then she opened her eyes and smiled.

I smiled too, from outside, with my chin on the snowy sill.

> Nobody in that cabin knew who stopped the wolves. The little girl only knew the medicine came home.

## The Yeti Way

**Help that nobody sees still counts all the way.**

The old man will probably tell people about a lucky snowslide. That is fine. I
was not doing it for the story. I was doing it because the pouch was on the
trail and I was the one looking at it.

I could not have moved that lip by myself. I did not need to. I only needed to
look at Papa Yeti, and he only needed to be the kind of Yeti who puts his hands
next to yours without asking why.

When we got back up to the ledge, Mama Yeti knelt in the snow and hugged me so
hard my curl got squashed. Papa Yeti kept a hand on my back. Nobody called me a
hero. Nobody had to. ❄️❤️
