It was the grey kind of afternoon that makes the whole mountain go quiet. I was
up in the pine clearing above the village, doing nothing in particular, which is
my favorite thing to do on a grey afternoon.

I did not see the farmer's boot snap the old stake on his path. I did not see
his goat look up from the ferns. What I heard was the ground under my feet
starting to hum.

## The hill let go

I pressed a hand flat against the nearest pine. The bark was trembling. Far
below, a whole slope had come loose and was roaring down over a hut. An empty
one, I learned later. Thank the mountain.

When that sound comes, there is no time to plan, and I am a Yeti who plans. So
I did the only thing that came to me. I picked the straightest pine in the
clearing, ran at it, and kicked.

It was a very good kick. I will say that once and never mention it again, which
Mama Yeti will tell you is a lie.

The pine went down with a crack that bounced off the ridge. I hauled it down to
the homestead just as another wave of earth broke loose above the farmer, still
sitting in the dirt where the first one had dropped him. I slammed the trunk
down between them and leaned on it with everything I had. The earth hit the wood
with a deep thump, piled up high, and stopped.

## What the hill was missing

The sun came out. The farmer stared at me with his mouth open. I stood there
feeling enormously pleased with myself, which lasted until I looked at the
slope.

It was bare. Moss, ferns, and loose brown soil, with almost no roots in it. And
up in the clearing stood the stump of the pine I had just broken. The hill had
slid because nothing was holding it, and my answer had been to knock down one
more thing that held.

> I had stopped a landslide with a tree. The least I could do was give the hill
> its tree back.

So I knelt in the loose soil and planted a young alder. The farmer dug in beside
me with a small iron trowel. The goat inspected every leaf. By the time the sun
went low and gold, four neighbors had strung rope lines across the slope and
were setting saplings into rings of cobbles, row after row.

The farmer pressed his palms together. I waved from the trunk and went home to
my dal.

## The Yeti Way

**A trunk can hold a hill for an afternoon, but only roots can hold it for good.**

The kick was the loud part. Everyone remembers the loud part. But a log jammed
in front of a moving hillside is a patch, not a fix. The fix was small and quiet
and had dirt all over it.

A family works the same way. The big rescue feels wonderful, and sometimes you
need one. What actually keeps the ground from sliding is slower: the small things
you plant on the ordinary days, while nothing is falling and nobody is watching.

Next spring I will walk down and check on those alders. I expect the goat will
have checked first.

Kick when you must. Plant every time. ❄️❤️
