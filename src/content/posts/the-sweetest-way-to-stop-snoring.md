Rain on the cave roof for three days. Not the loud kind. The steady kind that
makes the whole mountain feel tucked in with you.

Papa was snoring. He always snores when it rains. It starts as a hum, becomes a
saw, and lands somewhere around midnight on *yak clearing its throat inside a
cooking pot.* Two round piles of fur under one heavy blanket. Pink on the left,
blue on the right, warm all the way through.

## Then the blanket moved

Just a small puff, the way a tent breathes when the wind bumps it.

And a sound.

I will be a lady about this and say only that it came from the blue side of the
bed. At two in the morning.

My eyes opened. His eyes opened. The snoring, and I want this recorded properly,
**stopped immediately.** Three days of rain, four hours of sawing, and it turns
out the cure was free the whole time.

## The four seconds that matter

There is a little window that opens after something embarrassing happens between
two people. Four seconds, maybe five. Whatever you put in that window is what
your marriage learns to be.

You can put in a flinch. A sigh. A joke with a small knife in it. *Nice. Very
romantic. Thank you.* All available. Some Yetis build entire decades out of them.

We turned our heads at the same time. I giggled, because I couldn't not. And he,
this enormous blue creature who worries about everything and thinks he has to be
the strong one, just smiled back. No shame in it. No performance either. Just:
*yes, that was me, and you're still here, and isn't that funny.*

> The soft place in a marriage isn't the mattress. It's the four seconds after
> something goes wrong.

Then he reached up and gave the top edge of the blanket one polite little lift,
to let the mountain air do its work. No speech. No drama. A Yeti quietly handling
the situation he created, in the dark, at two in the morning.

So I scooted closer. Right into the warm blue side of the bed, fur against fur,
exactly where the problem had started four seconds earlier. The rain kept going.
Nobody said a word.

He was snoring again in about ninety seconds, by the way. The cure is powerful
but it does not last.

## The Yeti Way

**Love isn't the absence of embarrassing moments. It's what the two of you do in
the four seconds afterward.**

Nobody is impressive at two in the morning. Not the strongest Yeti on the tallest
peak. Sooner or later every one of us makes a sound we didn't plan to make, in
front of the one person whose opinion we actually care about.

So the question was never *how do I stay impressive.* It's **who do I want in the
room when I stop being impressive?**

The one who laughs with you. Who lifts the blanket instead of the eyebrow. Who
lets the whole silly moment pass through the room like weather, then pulls you
closer anyway. That is not a low standard. It is the highest one there is.

Laugh first. Lift the blanket. Scoot closer. ❄️❤️
