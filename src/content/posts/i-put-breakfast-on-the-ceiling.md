The oil was already talking when I picked up the pan, that low hiss that means
the stove is ready even when the cook is not.

I want it on record that I can fry an egg. Sunny side up, edges gone brown and
lacy, no drama. I have done it on a hundred mornings in this cave without a
single incident. That morning my wife was leaning on the counter with her tea,
watching me work, warm about it the way she is warm about most things I do
badly.

That was the problem. She was watching.

## The Flip Nobody Asked For

A man cooking alone gives the pan a small nudge. A man cooking for an audience
gives it a flick, and puts his whole shoulder into the flick, because somewhere
under all that fur he is still eighteen.

Clank. Swoosh. The egg went up past every height an egg has business reaching.

Then a wet smack from somewhere above my head, and the kitchen went very quiet.

I did not want to look up. I looked up. My breakfast was flat against the wooden
ceiling, yellow and glossy and holding on like it had signed a lease.

## What Came Down

This is where a sigh would have been fair. I had earned a sigh. I stood there
with an empty pan and a hot face, quietly doing the arithmetic on how tall a
ladder we own.

She put her paw on my shoulder instead, and patted it twice.

That is the moment the egg chose to let go. It came down without warning and
landed squarely on top of her head, in the good fur, the fur she fusses over.

> I have been married long enough to hear a sigh before it arrives. What arrived
> instead was a laugh so big she had to throw her head back to make room for it.

She laughed with yolk sliding toward her ear. I laughed because she was
laughing. Then I found the softest towel we own and cleaned her fur one slow
patch at a time, and neither of us said one word about breakfast, which by then
belonged to nobody.

## The Yeti Way

**A marriage is not scored on the mornings that go right. It is scored in the
half second after something goes wrong, when one of you decides what kind of
moment this is going to be.**

I have watched people spend that half second well and I have watched people
spend it badly, and the difference is not patience. It is speed. She got there
before I did. By the time my shame had its coat on, she had already turned the
whole thing funny, and there was nothing left for me to apologize to.

Anyone can be gentle about the egg that lands in the pan. The test is the egg on
the ceiling, and then the egg on your head, and whether you can still find the
joke while it is dripping.

Cook badly. Laugh first. Keep the towel soft. ❄️❤️
