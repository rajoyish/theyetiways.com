**Nanu:** I was scared of the dark for a really long time. Not baby-scared.
Real-scared. My room at night felt like it got bigger and I got smaller.

**Mama:** From my side, it was about forty minutes at every bedtime for most of
a year. I tried logic first — "there's nothing there" — which, it turns out, is
the least helpful sentence in the world to a scared kid.

## What didn't work

**Nanu:** People telling me it wasn't scary. It *was* scary. That's the whole
problem.

**Mama:** Rushing it. Every night I wanted to be done, and she could feel that I
wanted to be done, and that made it worse. My impatience was part of the dark.

## What worked

**Nanu:** Mama stopped trying to prove there was nothing there. Instead she'd
say, "I believe you that it feels scary, and I know you can handle it, and I'm
right down the hall." Then she'd actually go. And I'd be okay. And the next
night I was a little more okay.

**Mama:** I had to let her practice being brave, which meant I had to stop
rescuing her from every feeling. Confidence isn't something I could hand her. She
had to build it in the dark, a few minutes at a time, knowing I was close.

> Courage wasn't the absence of the scary feeling. It was doing bedtime anyway.

**Nanu:** It's not totally gone. But now the dark is just the dark. It's not a
monster. It's just the room with the lights off.
