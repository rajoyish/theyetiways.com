We get the joke a lot. *Big scary Yeti, must be freezing in there.* And sure,
the fur is thick and the mountain is cold. But the temperature inside a family
has nothing to do with the weather outside it.

## Warmth is a practice

I used to think closeness was something you either had or lost. Now I think it's
more like tending a fire. You don't get to light it once. You feed it, in small
ways, on the ordinary days when nothing is wrong.

> The days you don't feel like being warm are the days it counts double.

Here's what that looks like for Papa and me:

1. **We say the boring thing out loud.** "I'm tired and I'm not upset with you."
   It sounds silly. It prevents about half our arguments.
2. **We hand off, on purpose.** One of us is always allowed to be the one who's
   running low. Not both at once. That's the rule.
3. **We repair fast.** Not perfectly. Fast. A short "I snapped, sorry" within
   the hour beats a perfect apology three days later.

## The kids are watching the thermostat

Babu and Nanu don't remember what we say. They remember the temperature of the
room. If the grown-ups are warm to each other, the kids build their whole idea
of love on top of that. If we're cold, they learn that love is something you
survive.

So we practice. Cold world, warm ways. On purpose, every day.
