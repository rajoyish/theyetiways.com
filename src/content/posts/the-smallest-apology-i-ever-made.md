Third night of monsoon rain on the cave roof. Mama asleep on the pink side of the
bed. Me asleep on the blue side, doing what I do every wet night, which is snore
like a yak with strong opinions about the weather.

I sleep like a mountain. Nothing wakes me. That is more or less the whole story
of me. Big, warm, heavy, out cold by nine.

## Then the blanket puffed

I want it on the record that there was no plan. There is never a plan. It arrived
the way weather arrives, at two in the morning, under a blanket built for exactly
two Yetis.

My eyes opened. Her eyes opened.

And the snoring, four solid hours of it, stopped like somebody had pulled a plug
out of the wall.

I lay very still in the dark and did the arithmetic a Yeti does at that hour. She
is awake. She heard it. There is nowhere on this entire mountain for me to go.

## What I do with the rest of my life

I spend a great deal of energy being the one who handles things. I carry the
wood. I check the door twice. I sleep on the side nearer the cold so the draft
finds my back before it finds hers. It is my one skill and I am proud of it.

And in about half a second I had undone all of it with a sound.

She turned her head toward me and giggled. Not politely. She giggled the way Babu
giggles in a temple when he knows he shouldn't, all shoulders and no dignity.

So I did the only useful thing available to me. I reached up, lifted the top edge
of the blanket once, and let the cold mountain air in to do its work. No speech.
No *sorry, my love.* Just a Yeti quietly cleaning up after a Yeti.

> An apology is almost never a paragraph. It is one small action, done fast,
> without being asked.

She scooted straight over into my side. Fur against fur, back into the very spot
the trouble had come from, like the trouble had never been there at all. The rain
kept on. Neither of us said a word.

I was snoring again within two minutes. She has confirmed this.

## The Yeti Way

**Nobody stays loved by being impressive. You stay loved by what you do about the
unimpressive parts.**

For years I thought the job was to be the strong one, all the way through, with
no gaps. Then you make a noise at two in the morning and you find out that the
gaps were never the problem. Everyone has them. Every Yeti on every peak.

The problem is only ever what you do next. You can go stiff and pretend it wasn't
you. You can make a joke with a small blade in it before she can. Or you can lift
the blanket, take care of the thing you made, and let her laugh.

She wasn't laughing at me. I had that backwards for a long time. She was laughing
because we were both there, warm and silly and unbothered, in a bed with nothing
left to prove.

Own it. Lift the blanket. Go back to sleep. ❄️❤️
